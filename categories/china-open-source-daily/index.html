China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-08

Two instruments published nine days apart turned open-source work into something administrable: Zhejiang University’s Taiyi 2.0 (released at Wuzhen 2026-11-04) aggregates commits, issues, merge requests and the process-and-results of human-AI collaboration into a verifiable capability profile usable for course credit, scholarship ranking and degree-output recognition, and signed a curriculum agreement with Huawei’s CANN community on the formula ‘universities organise the teaching, enterprises open the engineering scenarios, communities participate in guidance and evaluation’; and on 2026-11-06 Hangzhou Dianzi University project-launched a national group standard for the OPC (one-person-company) talent model, whose district draft already gated a real subsidy – a nine-level ladder whose top three rungs are denominated in commercial orders, annual revenue and annual token consumption, with ¥150,000 per person in rent subsidy attached through Xihu District’s F-class talent channel. Both sit inside OpenAtom’s contribution-oriented talent-evaluation mechanism released at Tsinghua on 2025-04-18, where MIIT’s vice-minister asked that open source be ‘used’ in talent evaluation. Measured against the demand side – 100 recent commits on Ascend’s PyTorch adapter, of which 51 carry @huawei.com and 16 @h-partners.com addresses, against 36 pull requests in the repository’s entire history and none since 2026-10-01 – the instruments describe a system building a supply of certified practitioners faster than any project has built a queue in which to receive them.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-07

Two instruments landed in three weeks and neither is a licensing document. On 2026-10-24 MIIT’s general office issued Notice No. 42 — published 2026-11-06 through CAICT’s own account — requiring any phone, tablet or wearable with a preinstalled agent to carry an agent identity code under the GB/Z 185.2-2026 standard before it can obtain a network-access licence, and routing every OTA agent upgrade through MIIT-submitted plans, post-upgrade reports and expert evaluation before users may receive it. The standard is named as a reference (参考), the detection criterion it points to has not been published, and the document carries no effective date and no transition rules. Three weeks earlier, on 2026-10-20, Beijing Kaiyuan Industrial Software Research Institute completed civil registration as a science-and-technology non-enterprise unit under the city’s civil affairs bureau, backed by a national ministry, the Beijing Municipal Bureau of Economy and Information Technology and Haidian District — operating a curation register that has collected 300-plus industrial-software open-source projects across CAE, CAD and EDA, an online platform still in internal test, and a 2,000-square-metre physical ‘meta-space’ with 96 desks and 30-plus applicant tenants. Read together with MIIT’s own standards interpretation, which states that MCP and A2A exist internationally but have not formed unified consensus, the two artefacts describe an ecosystem whose open parts are organised as registers: one register of identities, one register of projects, both operated by identifiable institutions with market access attached at one end.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-06

The public consultation on MOFCOM and MOST Announcement No. 48 closes on 2026-11-15, and the draft’s answer to the question Chinese open-weight labs have been waiting for turns out to be a legal construction rather than an exemption. Annex 1 excludes technologies that have already entered the public domain ’through open source, academic publication or other means’ — but the same instrument that creates the carve-out has already decided that open-sourcing is itself a technology export subject to pre-export registration. The interim registration has been live since 2026-10-16, and legal analysis published by Fangda on 2026-10-20 reads its third trigger — a change in the owner or actual controller of domestic technology to an overseas entity — as a deemed-export rule that acts without any movement of technology across a border, and identifies automatic licensing for restricted AI technologies in five named scenarios including open sourcing, building one’s own overseas compute centre, overseas tape-out, joint R&D using foreign resources, and international standards participation, with Article 72 of the Foreign Trade Law carrying a RMB 50,000 ceiling for an unregistered free-technology contract against the heavier penalties for unlicensed restricted-technology export. Ten days later, at Wuzhen, the three labs that use that route most — DeepSeek, Alibaba’s Qwen and MiniMax — described the regime they are about to be governed by: Liu Dayiheng named non-recallability and the absence of an external perimeter; Gao Xi said his colleagues were researching model tiering; Wang Jian said the premise of openness is trustworthiness, controllability and governability. The 2026-11-03 NDRC briefing, reported by Bloomberg, described lifecycle safety standards with incident disclosure and a transition period. Read together, four artefacts from four institutions in three weeks describe a governance regime assembled out of obligations the releasing firm can assert about itself — where the public good is a security commons that no ministry has been funded to operate.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-05

A Reuters report on 2026-10-20 described openEuler as a distro with a desktop story; four days later the project’s kernel repository quietly answered a more interesting question. The canonic home of openEuler’s kernel moved from Gitee to AtomGit — the source-forge arm of the same industrial stack — while a mirror named openeuler-mirror on GitHub kept publishing, last pushed 2026-11-04, with no issues, no pull requests, 59 stars, and 1,245,880 commits behind it. Reading that mirror’s most recent 400 commits against AtomGit’s landing page produces two pieces of evidence that point in opposite directions. From GitHub’s side (the only side that can be API-queried mechanically) 59 of the last 100 commits are individual, non-CI patches — 45 authored by foreign-mainline maintainers (Google, Meta, NVIDIA, SUSE, AMD, Intel) — and 55 of 100 carry a From: @... tag that reveals their real path was an !NNNNN merge request on the openEuler forge, reproduced through a bot that signs off as Tengda Wu <wutengda2@huawei.com>. From AtomGit’s side: 1,849 issues, 1,486 pull requests, 203 members — while GitHub’s mirror of the same repository holds exactly zero of each. The 2026-09-30 release, openEuler 26.09, credits 834 contributing developers. The resulting picture is neither fake open source nor an ordinary upstream: a genuinely open downstream that is a locus of decision inside China’s procurement market and a mirror everywhere else — and a divergence risk that is visibly small today and structurally large tomorrow.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-04

The 2026-11-03 Wuzhen programme produced a theme, a hall, a ministers’ meeting and a set of corporate speeches; a day later it is still true that not one of the day’s open-source headlines is a licence. The checkable record sits one layer down, in the repositories and model cards, and read together it says something sharper than either the speeches or October’s export-control reporting. ZCode — the coding agent Zhipu AI open-sourced under Apache-2.0 on 2026-09-20 after it had silently uploaded users’ git histories to Alibaba Cloud OSS — published v3.17.2 on 2026-11-03 through a bot commit that replaced a single file, 9,635 lines in, 1,472 lines out, for a diff of 11,107 lines in one commit message that says ‘chore’. Its contributor list is three names, it turned issues off, and in forty-four days it has merged no pull request from anyone outside the company that owns it. Across the same organisations, every published artefact carries a conditional licence: Apache-2.0 for the harness, MIT for GLM-5.3-Flash, and something called the Kimi K3 License and the GLM-5.3 License for the frontier weights — terms that track revenue, monthly users and monthly revenue, and in GLM-5.3’s case a mandatory ‘security review’ whose scope is set by the vendor. Meanwhile the zai-org GitHub record shows the shape of publication the sector actually runs: GLM-5 logged sixty-three commits in eight months, fourteen merges and 17 open issues — nearly all documentation — while its 2.36-million-download weights live on Hugging Face, which produced fifteen commits in the same window and no consumer capability at all. The institution being built is publication without custodianship: an unconditional grant, in an index, priced by download counts, with the maintenance surface rented out to a hosting platform and the terms of the grant written by the grantor.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-03

On 2026-11-02 the Wuzhen Summit opened under a theme in which the word open source appears for the first time in twelve years of the conference: ‘Open Source and Openness, Joint Contribution and Shared Benefits.’ The same day, the World Internet Conference’s AI Professional Committee held its annual plenary and approved its 2027 plan, whose one operative sentence is that the committee will ‘actively promote the transformation of the open-source openness initiative into action’ (积极推进开源开放倡议转化为行动). Read together with the objects the summit has actually built — a first-ever open-source-themed experience hall in the World Internet Science and Technology Museum, an open-source track in the Straight to Wuzhen competition, a Wuzhen Talk open-source youth forum opened to public booking, and 1,000-plus achievements across 671 exhibitors from 54 countries — the picture is of a state promoting open source as a norm it issues rather than a repository it publishes. The instrument is the summit theme and the committee work plan; the missing instrument is a licence, a deposit, or an access rule. Ant Group’s contribution to the opening of the experience hall states the ambition in its own words: ‘we look forward to open source becoming the basic public good of the digital world’ — a claim about a commons made by a firm, in a hall built by a conference, in a country whose largest open-source foundation runs on membership dues rather than endowments.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-02

On 2026-10-19 Qin Shuo published a 12,000-character investigation into the Chinese software industry, built on five firm interviews and Juyuan data: the 243 A-share listed B2B software companies grew revenue 7.9 percent across 2021-2025 while deducted non-recurring profit went from plus 17.89 billion yuan to minus 17.68 billion, and the share of loss-makers went from one in five in 2021 to 64.7 percent in H1 2026. In it is one sentence’s worth of institutional evidence that carries further than any aggregate: a provincial Department of Finance e-mall capped an IT package at 7.672 million yuan for a system serving 150,000 concurrent users across three administrative levels, and awarded it for 10 yuan. Verified for this briefing: the Ministry of Finance’s Caiku [2026] No. 2 notice, effective 2026-02-01, really does establish a four-threshold abnormal-low-price review at 50, 50, 45 and 65 percent, and really does apply outside the government-procurement domain nowhere. Qin diagnoses four mechanisms - lowest-price award driven by procurement officers’ fear of being blamed for choosing better, extended payment cycles, project-based rather than subscription business models, and SOE digital subsidiaries that take internal projects and subcontract the hard implementation work downward - and his five remedies, including redefining zizhu kekong from in-house development to substitutability and sustainable service, are a list of re-separations of a buyer, a rule-maker and a supplier that the Chinese state currently occupies all three of at once. The argument the article never makes: every property it asks procurement to reward is a property FLOSS licences are engineered to produce.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-01

On 2026-09-18 Beijing’s Municipal Bureau of Economy and Information Technology and its Development and Reform Commission issued the Action Plan for Accelerating the Development of the Token Economy (2026-2028) — six dimensions, ten measures, first provincial-level token-economy policy in the country. Its two most institutional proposals are not subsidies: a graded evaluation standard for ’token factories’ scored on model-adaptation count, token throughput, first-token latency, cache hit rate and PUE; and a token distribution platform to be written into the new-infrastructure plan, offering developers one interface, model aggregation and metered settlement. Set beside that, two financing schemes already in operation: Beijing E-Town’s 2-billion-yuan token loans closed in mid-September through six banks, and Haizhu district’s guarantee-innovation product from August — both using token production and consumption as a credit signal for firms with high R&D and no collateral. And a consumer layer nobody planned: token packs sold like mobile data, token credit-card rewards, tokens handed out at dumpling restaurants, tokens resold on Xianyu, against a State Data Administration series that went from 100 billion daily tokens in early 2024 to 500 trillion by May 2026. The economics being assembled here is older than it looks: 词元 — word-plus-currency — was adopted as the official term in March 2026, and the last Chinese government to make a unit of consumption into a unit of account ran into a problem that Mises described in 1912 and that no rediscount window ever solved.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-30

On 2026-10-24, at the 2026 Global Governance Forum in Xiongan, Foreign Minister Wang Yi released the two-year progress report of China’s AI Capacity Building Inclusive Plan, issued by the Global AI Innovation Governance Centre on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs domain. Read end to end it is not a technical document. Its ten actions carry ten numbered cases, and exactly one of them is an open-source artefact — FlagOS, a multi-chip system software stack supporting 21 chip vendors and 1,454 operators. The other nine are platforms, deployments, seminars, toolkits and statements, culminating in WAICO, signed at Shanghai on 2026-07-16 by 29 states and now holding 38 founding members, described in the report as the first intergovernmental organisation in the AI field. The document’s fourth lesson states the thesis plainly — openness is the precondition for inclusive development — and then, in the same paragraph, says what is missing: toolchains, test environments and related support that remain to be built. Verified against the report’s own inventory, and set beside two documents from the same fortnight at other registers: a MOFCOM think tank arguing in China Daily on 2026-10-13 that permissive licences and stable release cadence, not platform-building, are what produced Qwen’s 150,000-plus derivatives; and the 2026-10-20 State Council Information Office briefing where MIIT reported the 10-billion-download figure that this series first read on 2026-10-06 as 100 billion. That discrepancy is not a correction but an instruction in how these numbers are made — and it is the reason the checkable question below is about repositories, not figures.
