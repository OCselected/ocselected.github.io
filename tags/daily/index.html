China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-25

On 2026-10-23 Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro to its own API, its apps and its enterprise customers — and announced that the weights will follow on 2026-11-04. The model is 2.8 trillion total parameters, activates 104 billion, and carries a one-million-token context window; by the parameter count it is the largest open-weight model in the world. Three days earlier, CCF’s Open Source Development Technical Committee convened a seminar in Chengdu at which its chair, Academician Wang Huaimin of NUDT, judged that Chinese open source has entered the ‘participating and integrating’ stage, that open source is a strategic fulcrum of AI innovation and technological competition, and that the road to ‘global open-source leadership’ still runs through bottlenecks in platform-grade projects, international communities and long-term mechanisms. In a separate and much smaller item, a Reddit count of the twenty-three projects that smartphones and browsers actually run on found that eleven of them had been maintained over the past year by one or two people — xz by one, at 97 percent of its changes, with no confirmed funding since the 2024 backdoor. Read together, these three items locate the binding constraint: at the top of the parameter curve weights are becoming conditional artefacts published by the largest firms on their own schedules, and at the bottom of the stack the software nobody can do without is held by one person per project.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-24

On the evening of 2026-10-22, Yuyuantantian — the new-media arm of CCTV — reported that DeepSeek and MiniMax had both 顺利履行登记手续, successfully discharged the pre-export registration formalities, and completed 开源和海外授权使用, open sourcing and overseas licensing. Seven days earlier, Announcement No. 48’s exemption for technology ‘already in the public domain by means of open sourcing’ had been a clause on paper; as of that evening it is a clause with two named firms’ registration records behind it and a documented outcome. The next morning, 2026-10-23, MiniMax released the weights of M3.1 Flash — 428B total parameters, 23B active — on Hugging Face and ModelScope, and its Hong Kong shares rose more than 16 percent while Zhipu gained over 8. Read together with the two documents from the same fortnight — a 32-plus-16-member agent open-source community being recruited by CAICT for a third intake closing 2026-11-06, and the NPC Standing Committee’s 2026-11-02 agenda item on approving the agreement establishing the World AI Cooperation Organization — three events in three days describe a single institutional arrangement: the permission step has moved off the licence and onto the release form, and the state has begun to operate it as a channel rather than as a gate.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-23

On 2026-10-16 MOFCOM and MOST published Announcement No. 48 of 2026, proposing 40 additions to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted from Export — 23 of them AI — and simultaneously switching on a pre-export registration regime that took effect the day of publication, before any consultation had closed. Read in the original Chinese, the clause that matters is six characters long: technologies that have already entered the public domain 「通过开源、发表学术论文等方式」 are not covered. That sentence makes open sourcing a lawful exit from an export-control regime, and it is the first time a Chinese industrial-control instrument has been written with so open a back door — installed, as it happens, in the same window in which DeepSeek raised about RMB 100 billion (roughly USD 15 billion) and open-sourced DeepInfer, the production inference engine behind its own API, under Apache 2.0, with Huawei’s Ascend 950 listed alongside Nvidia SM100 on the first day. Three events, eleven days, one mechanism: the act of publishing has become the most valuable move available to a Chinese AI firm, and the state has just decided to keep the door open.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-22

Two documents from the same week describe two different ways an institution can accumulate authority over a technical field, and the distance between them is the finding. On 2026-10-20 the OSCAR Trustworthy Open Source account published the programme for the 2026 OSCAR Open Source Industry Conference (Beijing, 2026-11-10), hosted by the China Communications Standards Association and organised by CAICT, whose six headline items are almost entirely apparatus rather than findings: the release of a trust-evaluation result set, the launch of a six-networks open-IoT standards system, the founding of an AI Infra software expert group, two new China Institute of Communications standards (a transparency grading method for open LLMs and a governance maturity model for enterprise open-LLM adoption), and the annual research reports. Five days earlier, openEuler occupied the largest distro booth at Open Source Summit Europe 2026 in Prague, presented a SuperPoD OS and agentic-scaling roadmap in a Technical Committee keynote, and — in the one detail that carries the institutional weight — is a downstream that diverged rather than a downstream that reconstructed. The two artefacts are read here side by side because they are rival mechanisms: one manufactures standing through assessment and standardisation, the other through having been forced to stop depending on upstream.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-21

At its 2026-10-20 weekly meeting, the China OSS Promotion Union (COPU), the country’s oldest open-source industry body (founded 2004-07-22 as a non-independent legal-person voluntary association under government-department guidance), tabled a restructuring outline that leaves its stated international posture untouched while re-drawing its membership boundary: ~140 domestic member units to be verified and re-admitted, 24 foreign member organisations listed for expulsion — IBM, Intel, Google, Microsoft, Red Hat, Ubuntu/Canonical, SAP, GitHub, ARM, VMware, Mozilla, Debian and others — and DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM and MiniMax named for development. Read against COPU’s own June 2026 “one body, two wings” strategy, in which the western wing is defined as deep participation in European and American rule governance and leadership of next-generation standards, the document applies a national-membership filter to an organisation whose declared comparative advantage is sitting in western standards rooms — without naming a single alternative channel through which those 24 units’ technical engagement would continue.
