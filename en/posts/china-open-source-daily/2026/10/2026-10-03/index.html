3. WIC (@wicinternet) — World Internet Conference news conference: theme and key activities of the 2026 WIC Wuzhen Summit, announced by Secretary-General Ren Xianliang

The World Internet Conference (WIC, 世界互联网大会) announced on September 30, 2026 that the 2026 WIC Wuzhen Summit will take place from November 2 to 5 in Wuzhen, Zhejiang Province, and — per the announcement — will place open-source AI and international cooperation on AI development and governance at the center of its agenda. The announcement was made at a news conference in Beijing by WIC Secretary-General Ren Xianliang.

The sharpest institutional-economics fact of the day: the summit-plus-27-sub-forums-plus-Global-Governance-Initiative-plus-community-with-shared-future-in-cyberspace pairing at a single summit-preparatory-announcement. Per the CGTN report:

  • The summit will “bring together Chinese and international participants for discussions on innovation, security governance, cultural exchanges and international cooperation.”
  • “A dialogue at the main forum will gather executives from leading technology companies and developers of open-source large models to discuss how to build a global open-source AI ecosystem.”
  • The summit will feature 27 sub-forums with topics including trust, connectivity and interoperability among AI agents, as well as robotics, embodied AI and spatiotemporal intelligence.
  • “Cyberspace cooperation under the Global Governance Initiative will also feature in the discussions” — the Global Governance Initiative (G20/GI) is the Chinese-government-sponsored international-diplomatic platform.
  • “The theme of this year’s Wuzhen Summit emphasizes open source, inclusiveness, joint contribution and shared benefits in building a community with a shared future in cyberspace.”
  • “Discussions will address both the principles and practical applications of open-source and inclusive AI development, as well as international concerns over its governance.”
  • Other events include the WIC Awards for Pioneering Science and Technology, the WIC Distinguished Contribution Award, the Light of Internet Expo, and presentations of outstanding cases of jointly building a community with a shared future in cyberspace.
  • The summit “follows broader Chinese efforts to promote international collaboration in AI. An action plan issued at the World Artificial Intelligence Conference in Shanghai in July called for sharing open-source ecosystems, jointly developing rules and standards and strengthening cooperation on AI safety governance.”

The Jamestown Foundation’s September 2026 analysis documents that “Beijing is converging on a ’tiered governance’ approach to open weight models, under which basic capabilities would be released freely, frontier capabilities would face security review, and the most sensitive models would be confined to only domestic use.” Per Jamestown: “Support for open weights is a deliberate response to constraints.” Per Jamestown: “Open weights serve Beijing’s security priorities, which treat sovereignty and development as core components of its expansive view of national security. Possession of the weights is what allows government, financial, and military users to run models offline on classified data, free from a foreign provider that could cut off access at any time.”

The sharpest institutional-economics fact of the day: the three-tier open-source-control proposal by Chen Bing (Nankai University) at the Supreme People’s Court roundtable. Per Jamestown: “The idea of a tiered classification system is beginning to gain traction. At the Supreme People’s Court roundtable mentioned above, Chen Bing of Nankai University proposed a three-tier scheme of controls. Under Chen’s proposal, basic and general-purpose open-source technology would be handled through a regulatory filing process; strategic and frontier open-source technology would face substantive export controls and be required to undergo security assessment; and core technology bearing on national security would in principle not be open-sourced at all, or would be opened only in a limited way within a strictly controlled scope.”

The institutional-object layer that this is a first-documented instance in this series of: the Chinese-open-weight-model tiered-governance scholarly layer. Per Jamestown: “Beijing has not left open weight models entirely uncontrolled. Several prominent experts have argued in recent years for tiered and classified regulation, including Zhang Huibin, Zhou Hui (周辉) of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, who led the drafting group for the Model Law on Artificial Intelligence (人工智能示范法); and Zhang Linghan (张凌寒), director of the Institute of AI Law at China University of Political Science and Law.” Per Jamestown quoting Zhang Huibin and Xu Lei (June 2026): “Zhang calls this approach ‘using technology to carry rules’ (以技术带规则) and appreciates that it is ‘more sustainable and more persuasive than exporting rules on their own’ (比单纯的规则输出更具可持续性和说服力).” Per Jamestown: “For now, such a system remains a conversation among legal scholars and in the Party’s mouthpiece, but it appears to be gaining support.”
