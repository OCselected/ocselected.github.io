China Open Source Daily — 2026-10-15#

🏛️ Policy & Regulation — CPC Central Committee plus State Council’s October 9, 2026 Joint “Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces” Documents the Chinese-Technology-Innovation Policy-Formation Surface at Its Highest-Institutional-Authority Layer Simultaneously with the “AI Plus” Initiative plus National-Blockchain-Network Construction plus “Involution-Style Competition” Ban plus Private-Enterprise-Leads-Major-National-Tech-Tasks Institutional-Form Layer#

1. The Communist Party of China Central Committee (CPC, 中共中央) and the State Council of the People’s Republic of China (中华人民共和国国务院) — joint “Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces” (《关于加快发展新质生产力的意见》) issued October 9, 2026, released publicly October 10, 2026.

2. Institutional placement — issued jointly by the CPC Central Committee plus the State Council — the highest-institutional-authority form of policy issuance in the Chinese policy formation hierarchy, alongside (but not subordinated to) the Politburo or Central Committee Plenum.

3. Structural composition — 19 key measures across five main areas:

  • (a) Advancing scientific and technological innovation — original and transformational sci-tech innovation; major sci-tech projects targeting strategic, landmark, and leading products at the cutting edge of global science and technology.
  • (b) Deepening the integration of sci-tech and industrial innovation — high-quality development of key manufacturing industrial chains; industrial innovation projects in priority areas; coordinated efforts on national strategic sci-tech capabilities via national laboratories.
  • (c) Innovating development approaches — the principal role of enterprises in sci-tech innovation; technology-based small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) increase R&D investment; capable private enterprises support major national technological breakthrough tasks; use-first, pay-later licensing by universities and research institutions to MSMEs.
  • (d) Promoting institutional and mechanism innovation — corporate R&D reserve system; increased additional tax deduction rate for tech-oriented SME R&D expenses; enterprise R&D projects meeting national strategic needs elevated to national projects through prescribed procedures; coordinated research and commercialization tasks at project initiation with actual-application-results assessment upon completion.
  • (e) Improving mechanisms for talent development — coordinated allocation of national sci-tech research institutions, high-level research universities, and leading sci-tech enterprises.

4. Core technical-institutional provisions.

Provision 1: “AI Plus” initiative (人工智能+). The guidelines call for “fully implementing the AI Plus initiative, using AI to transform traditional industries, accelerating innovation in AI and other digital and intelligent technologies, and promoting the widespread application of AI across industries.” The guidelines also call for “establishing systems for technology monitoring, early risk warning and emergency response to ensure that AI is safe, reliable and controllable.”

Provision 2: National blockchain network construction. Among the 19 measures is a call to “construct a national blockchain network alongside a nationwide integrated computing power network.” Per Crypto Briefing’s October 12, 2026 analysis: “The guidelines contain no explicit mention of independent digital assets, crypto tokens, permissionless systems or trading activity. The blockchain in this plan is state-coordinated infrastructure.” The blockchain provision ties into the 2020-established Blockchain-based Service Network (BSN, 区块链服务网络) and MIIT’s 2021 blockchain guidance.

Provision 3: National data infrastructure. The guidelines call for “optimizing the layout, construction and operation of national data infrastructure, advancing the digital transformation of the manufacturing sector, and building digital industrial clusters with international competitiveness,” alongside an “open, shared and secure nationally integrated data market.”

5. Structural-institutional provisions — the anti-involution clause. The guidelines explicitly call for “cross-regional industrial cooperation to avoid involution-style competition” (避免内卷式竞争) — an explicitly named domestic-institutional-form prohibition targeting local-government-driven subsidy wars and duplicate capacity expansion across Chinese provinces. Localities must “tailor their approaches to actual conditions”; the guidelines warn against “speculative expansion, industrial hollowing-out and blindly pursuing new industries” and prohibit “the improper use of policies in the name of developing new quality productive forces,” with “serious accountability in cases where investment stampedes or blind investment cause major losses.”

6. Structural-institutional provisions — patient capital and foreign VC. The guidelines seek “stronger patient capital support for early-stage, small and hard-technology businesses” and call for “encouraging foreign venture capital institutions, international standards organizations and others to develop in China,” alongside “actively building a global innovation cooperation network.”

7. Institutional placement within the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030). Per the guidelines’ framing: “The outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) identifies the development of new quality productive forces as a key strategic task and sets out 28 major projects related to this goal, accounting for 25.7 percent of the 109 major projects outlined in the plan.” The 28 projects focus on four priorities: strengthening industrial capabilities and competitiveness; fostering emerging industries; pursuing breakthroughs in cutting-edge technologies; and enhancing innovation capabilities.

8. Key institutional-economics facts of the day — a first-documented instance at the CPC-plus-State-Council-plus-19-measures-plus-five-areas layer. Per Xinhua / CCTV’s October 10 report (Wang Shengxiao, researcher with the Party School of the CPC Central Committee): “Seizing this historic opportunity requires moving faster to achieve greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology and raising innovation capacity across the board to maintain strategic initiative.” Per Liu Zhenzhong, researcher at the Institute of Industrial and Technological Economics of the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC): “New quality productive forces are primarily driven by transformational technological breakthroughs, which bring profound changes to how innovation is organized, industries evolve and resources are allocated.”

9. Quantitative context — high-tech manufacturing growth. Per the National Bureau of Statistics: “China’s high-tech manufacturing sector has maintained strong growth. Its value-added output among large enterprises rose 16.7 percent year-on-year in August, compared with 5.2 percent growth in overall industrial output. Industrial robot production increased 34.6 percent.”

The CPC Central Committee plus State Council’s October 9, 2026 “Opinions on Developing New Quality Productive Forces” is the first-documented instance in this series of the Chinese-technology-innovation policy-formation institutional object being operationalized at the CPC-plus-State-Council-plus-19-measures-plus-five-areas highest-institutional-authority policy layer. The document’s placement at the CPC-plus-State-Council joint-issuance layer — not subordinated to any ministry-level or provincial-level policy surface — places the Chinese technology-innovation policy-formation institutional object at a level this series has tracked at the state-adjacent-research-institute layer (CAICT, CCID Institute) and at the ministry-level layer (MIIT, NDRC, Cyberspace Administration) but has not documented at this level of institutional authority before.

Bank of America’s withdrawal is the most recently-documented change to Moonshot’s underwriter syndicate, following the bank’s earlier reported inclusion in the syndicate in the September 4, 2026 filing announcement.

3. The Moonshot IPO underwriter syndicate composition timeline.

  • September 4, 2026 — initial confidential filing announcement. Per Bloomberg (Dong Cao, Julia Fioretti, Pei Li, Luz Ding): Moonshot AI is considering raising up to $5 billion in a Hong Kong IPO as soon as this year. The Beijing lab has “filed confidentially and added Bank of America as overall coordinator alongside sponsor banks China International Capital Corporation, Deutsche Bank and Goldman Sachs.”
  • September 10, 2026 — Reuters reports Moonshot exploring dual Hong Kong plus Shanghai STAR listing.
  • October 6, 2026 — Reuters and Yahoo Finance report Moonshot targets a $50 billion valuation for the Hong Kong IPO in early 2027, with Bloomberg reporting parallel documentation of DeepSeek’s Tencent-backed fundraising.
  • October 11, 2026 — Reuters exclusive on Moonshot executives’ settlement talks with Recurrent.ai investors ahead of the Hong Kong IPO. Per this series’ October 14 briefing: Recurrent.ai and some of its investors accuse Moonshot CEO Yang Zhilin and CTO Zhang Yutao of making false representations to Recurrent investors and enriching themselves at Recurrent’s expense, with a March 11, 2026 letter seeking compensation equivalent to 10.5 percent of Moonshot’s original shares (worth more than $700 million based on a June funding round).
  • October 14, 2026 — Bloomberg exclusive: Bank of America drops out of the underwriter arrangement talks.

4. The TNW September-to-October 2026 syndicate documentation — the institutional interpretation of the syndicate composition. Per The Next Web (October 2026): “Look at who is reported to be arranging it. Bank of America, Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank are not Chinese institutions, and their presence on a Beijing AI champion’s listing says something about where the wall actually runs. Export control governs what American companies may sell. It does not govern what American banks may underwrite in Hong Kong, and the fee pool for a $5bn listing is substantial.” Per TNW: “The constraint was applied to hardware and the money was left alone.”

5. The competitive-institutional context — Zhipu AI and MiniMax Hong Kong IPOs. Per TNW: “Zhipu AI and MiniMax debuted on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on 8 and 9 January [2026], seeking $560m and up to $539m respectively, as Kinling Lo reported for Rest of World. Both were oversubscribed, and both went public before OpenAI or Anthropic had filed. Chinese labs reached the public markets first, which is not the sequence anyone in Washington sketched out in 2022.” Moonshot’s contemplated listing would be “considerably larger than either” — several times the combined raise size of Zhipu and MiniMax.

6. The compute-institutional context — Nvidia hardware despite export controls. Per TNW: “Moonshot trained its flagship model on Nvidia silicon regardless. Kimi K3 was trained on a 20,000-chip cluster supplied through Alibaba’s cloud, with multiple accounts pointing to H200 accelerators.” Per TNW: “Moonshot billed K3 as the world’s largest open-weight model at 2.8 trillion parameters when it launched in July.”

7. The valuation-and-revenue trajectory. Per TNW: “The numbers have been revised upwards all year, which is its own signal. TNW reported a planned listing within six months at a $30bn valuation, and a subsequent double raise chasing $50bn. Demand is the stated driver, with annual recurring revenue reported at around $300m and subscriptions paused at one point because the company ran short of GPUs.”
