📚 Policy Research — CCID Institute’s 2026-10-13 Reprint of Xu Jia + Yang Chenling + Wang Yuyue’s “我国信息技术产业如何走出合作新路” Documents the National-Institute-Policy-Research-Paper 4x5 Capital-Formation Diagnosis Layer: Government-Guidance-Fund-Cluster-Plus-Industrial-CVC-Plus-Financial-Innovation-Plus-Cross-Border-Capital Formation at the State-Adjacent-Research-Institute Layer#

1. China Academy of Information and Communications Technology (CCID, 赛迪智库, directly under the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology) — Informatization and Software Industry Research Institute, authors Xu Jia + Yang Chenling + Wang Yuyue (徐嘉 + 杨辰凌 + 王越) — “我国信息技术产业如何走出合作新路” (How China’s IT Industry Can Find a New Path of Cooperation), originally published in the Software and Integrated Circuits magazine September 2026 issue, reprinted to WeChat on 2026-10-13 — 4,820 Chinese characters — four levels of current-state documentation plus four challenges plus five policy responses.

2. The four current-state layers.

Layer A: Government-guidance-fund cluster scale expansion plus regional-acceleration. Anhui’s 1+16+N architecture government equity investment funds exceed 300 billion yuan, with 16 mother funds establishing 198 sub-funds where the provincial fiscal capital of 14.68 billion yuan leveraged 78.23 billion yuan social capital. Shanghai’s three-leading-industry mother funds third batch of 17 sub-funds covering artificial intelligence and integrated circuits, with mother-fund planned capital of 4.71 billion yuan and sub-fund total scale of 22.89 billion yuan — a 4.86x leverage ratio. Guangdong’s provincial-industry-development-investment fund has established a hundred-billion-scale AI-and-robotics industry investment fund cluster, with the 2026 first-half AI industry investment fund expected total scale of approximately 20 billion yuan.

Layer B: Industrial capital CVC accelerating rise. Three documented CVC archetypes: (i) Lenovo Venture as a platform-type CVC with cumulative investments exceeding 300 companies and cultivation of 60-plus national-level “little giant” (专精特新小巨人) enterprises; (ii) Xiaomi-system supply-chain-integration-type CVC arranged around the human-car-home all-ecosystem covering intelligent electric vehicles, automotive electronics, intelligent sensing, power batteries, autonomous driving, and intelligent manufacturing; (iii) Tencent Investment as a digital-ecosystem-coordination-type CVC加码 StepFun (阶跃星辰) and Moonshot AI (月之暗面) and driving the integration with Tencent Cloud and WeChat ecosystem. The three CVC types correspond to a gradient of ecosystem enabling, vertical deep cultivation, and strategic slot-taking.

Layer C: Financial-service innovation. China Standard Bank (标准银行)’s Agentar knowledge-engineering platform lifted complex-question-answering accuracy from 68% to 91% and response time into the hundred-millisecond range, selected as a 2025 international-standard financial-application excellence case. FinTech One账通 (金融壹账通) integrated 370-plus authoritative data sources covering ten domains and enabled 20-plus insurance institutions with economic and social benefits exceeding 10 billion yuan. Haier鑫海汇数科 (Haier Xinhui Shu Ke) landed blockchain plus smart-contract plus AI-intelligent-algorithm in asset-isolated warehousing service trust with business scale reaching 1 billion yuan.

Layer D: Cross-border industrial-financial combination. Hesteel Group (河钢集团) jointly with China International Capital Corporation (CICC, 中金资本) established the CICC Hesteel Hebei Development Equity Investment Fund with a total scale of 32 billion yuan, an operation period of 15 years, and Abu Dhabi’s BlueFive Capital as a participating investor. Hansen Software (汉森软件) received CSRC overseas issuance listing filing on 2026-04. Ruiwei Information (瑞为信息) received CSRC overseas issuance listing filing on 2026-05. Wanka Yiyi (万咖壹) received a strategic investment of 350 million Hong Kong dollars.

3. The four challenges.

Challenge 1: Early-stage hard-tech investment-aversion. Market capital has a natural disinclination to invest in early hard-tech and basic-software fields where the technical route has not been validated and the commercialization prospects are unclear. A nationally unified authoritative IP and technology-achievement assessment and transaction circulation market has not yet matured, and the assessment system is absent, forming a vicious cycle of dare-not-invest and cannot-invest.

Challenge 2: Capital-supply short-termification plus bubble-tendency co-existence. IT industry capital supply exhibits a significant dumbbell-type divergence — early project count is high but per-round amounts are tightening, and mid-to-late-stage capital concentrates into a small number of head projects. Fields requiring long-term tackling — basic software, industrial software, other hard-bone hard-tech — face capital shortage.

Challenge 3: Traditional-financial-service-system capability-deficit plus incentive-misalignment. The lifetime-responsibility institutional constraint on non-performing loans has not fundamentally reversed the micro-credit-staff dare-not-lend and do-not-want-to-lend phenomenon. The supply of hybrid talent who understand both technology and finance is severely insufficient.

Challenge 4: Tech-company governance shortcomings versus capital-market compliance requirements. A chasm exists between tech-company governance structures and the compliance requirements of the capital markets.

4. The five policy responses.

Policy 1: Strengthen long-term capital supply, optimize capital structure. Push pension funds, insurance capital, and other long-term institutional investors to increase support for strategic emerging industries. Establish a national-strategic-science fund focused on breakthrough technology fields. Loosen return-investment requirements and extend fund duration.

Policy 2: Improve technical-element-market infrastructure, resolve assessment-and-pricing difficulties. Accelerate the construction of a national-unified technology transaction market and IP transaction platform. Push data-asset securitization. Improve the technology-risk-assessment system.

Policy 3: Innovate financial-service tools, improve industrial-financial adaptation. Expand REITs application in new-type infrastructure fields such as data centers and computing-infrastructure. Develop investment-loan linkage, option loan, IP pledge financing, and supply-chain finance. Push financial institutions to establish tech-finance dedicated institutions or business divisions. Break the lifetime-responsibility incentive distortion on grassroots staff, truly realizing the shift from looking-at-the-past to looking-at-the-future credit logic.

Policy 4: Improve corporate governance and compliance capability. Push equity-architecture clarification, decision-mechanism standardization, and financial-information transparency. Strengthen tech-company and capital-market training and connection. Push enterprises to establish complete IP management systems.

Policy 5: Construct an open-plus-secure cross-border industrial-financial system. Deepen cooperation with Middle-East and Southeast-Asia sovereign wealth funds and international capital. Improve cross-border industrial-financial linkage legal framework and risk-prevention mechanism. Support tech companies through offshore markets such as China Hong Kong to achieve internationalization financing.
