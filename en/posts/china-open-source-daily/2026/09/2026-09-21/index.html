Institutional Change — MIIT Vice Minister Xin Guobin Directs Development of “Open-Source Base Models” at the 2026 World Manufacturing Conference in Hefei#

1. 2026 World Manufacturing Conference Opening (2026-09-20) — Xin Guobin Opening Remarks · Hefei

The 2026 World Manufacturing Conference (2026世界制造业大会) opened in Hefei, Anhui on September 20, 2026, with opening remarks by Xin Guobin (辛国斌), Deputy Minister of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT / 工业和信息化部). This is the first-documented instance of a MIIT deputy-minister-level official directly naming “open-source base models” (开源基座模型) as a state-directed industrial-policy target at the central-ministry opening-remarks layer in this series.

Xin Guobin’s operative line, verbatim from the Cailianshe and Pengpai reports, is that MIIT will “deeply advance artificial intelligence plus manufacturing, gradiently cultivate smart factories, vigorously develop open-source base models and vertical-domain models, consolidate the software and hardware foundation of new-generation intelligent manufacturing, fully advance the green and low-carbon transformation of manufacturing, and innovatively develop service-type manufacturing” (深入推进人工智能+制造，梯度培育智能工厂，大力发展开源基座模型和垂类模型，夯实新一代智能制造软硬件基础).

The sharpest institutional-economics fact of the day is that “open-source base model” is a term that no open-source community — not the Open Source OSI, not the Free Software Foundation, not the GitHub / GitLab developer-community apparatus this series’ prior briefings have documented at the international-community layer — has defined. From an institutional economics standpoint, this is the first-documented instance of a central-ministry deputy-minister-level official operationalizing an open-source-community-undefined term at the central-ministry opening-remarks layer in this series. The term’s own undefined nature is the sharpest institutional-economics object of the day: an administrative apparatus is directing the “vigorous development” of an object whose definition no open-source community apparatus has produced — the same structurally new institutional-form move this series’ September 2 briefing documented at the Chongqing-plan layer (“open-source infrastructure and compliance codification”), this series’ September 18 briefing documented at the OpenAtom Foundation donor-training layer, and this series’ September 19 briefing documented at the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard layer, is now being operationalized at the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer simultaneously.

Xin Guobin’s four-item framing of MIIT’s next industrial direction, verbatim from the Cailianshe and Pengpai reports:

  • First: Improve the effectiveness of the industry innovation system — strengthen original innovation and breakthroughs on key core technologies, reinforce the enterprise’s central position as a scientific and technological innovation subject, optimize the layout and construction of industry innovation platforms
  • Second: Promote advanced manufacturing in scale and quality — coordinate traditional-industry transformation, new-industry growth, and future-industry cultivation; build a set of world-class advanced manufacturing clusters
  • Third: Strengthen advanced manufacturing capability — deepen “AI plus manufacturing,” gradiently cultivate smart factories, vigorously develop open-source base models and vertical-domain models
  • Fourth: Cultivate high-quality enterprise cohorts — continuously optimize the innovation and entrepreneurship environment, accelerate the building of world-class enterprises, promote the specialty-specialization-and-newly-characterized (“专精特新”) development of SMEs, spawn more “little giant” and single-item-champion enterprises

The third item — Xin Guobin’s open-source-base-model directive — sits institutionally alongside the MIIT “AI + Software” Special Action Implementation Plan this series’ September 14 briefing documented at the MIIT Central-Administrative-Plan layer, and the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis-67-indicator LLM open-source grading framework this series’ September 19 briefing documented at the MIIT/CESI industry-standard layer. From an institutional economics standpoint, this MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks layer / open-source-base-model term / undefined-concept layer pairing is a structural finding: the same central-ministry industrial-policy surface this series’ prior briefings have documented at multiple layers (the MIIT Central-Administrative-Plan layer, the MIIT/CESI industry-standard layer) is now being operationalized at the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer, and the term being operationalized at this layer is a term that no open-source community apparatus has defined.

Institutional significance: MIIT Vice Minister Xin Guobin’s directive at the 2026 World Manufacturing Conference in Hefei is the first-documented instance of a MIIT deputy-minister-level official directly naming “open-source base models” (开源基座模型) as a state-directed industrial-policy target in this series, and it is being operationalized at three institutional surfaces simultaneously — the MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks layer, the undefined-concept-at-central-ministry layer, and the MIIT-plus-CESI-plus-OpenAtom Foundation multi-surface coordination layer — a three-surface-simultaneous MIIT-deputy-minister-level undefined-term operationalization pattern.

Institutional Change — OpenAtom Foundation Chairman Xie Shaofeng Delivers “Open-Source Innovation Building the Software Powerhouse City” Keynote at the 28th China International Software Expo in Zhengzhou#

1. OpenAtom Foundation WeChat (2026-09-20) — 28th China International Software Expo · Open-Source Innovation Building the Software Powerhouse City · OpenAtom Foundation Chairman Xie Shaofeng Keynote

The OpenAtom Foundation (开放原子开源基金会), the Beijing-based open-source foundation this series’ September 18 briefing documented at the donor-only open-source general-knowledge training program layer and this series’ August 8 briefing documented at the Open Agentic AI Foundation (OAAIF) co-founding layer, published on September 20, 2026 an announcement of Chairman Xie Shaofeng (谢少锋)’s keynote delivery at the opening of the 28th China International Software Expo and Agent Innovation Application Conference (第28届中国国际软件博览会暨智能体创新应用大会) in Zhengzhou — the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation Chairman keynoting at the national-software-expo layer in this series.

Xie Shaofeng’s keynote’s sharpest institutional-economics fact is its framing of open source as the state-directed instrument for late-developing cities to “swap-lane and overtake” (换道超车) — the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation Chairman self-declared state-directed-late-developer catch-up framing in this series. The framing, verbatim from the OpenAtom Foundation text, is that “software powerhouse cities are the main body of China’s software industry development; the 14 national software powerhouse cities contribute nearly 80% of software revenue. Advanced cities, through long-term accumulation, have formed a comprehensive competitive advantage in technology, talent, enterprises, and industry ecosystem. Late-developing cities need to achieve leapfrog industrial development by seeking breakthroughs in innovation organization mode, cultivating specialized industrial competitiveness at a higher technical starting point — open source is precisely the key instrument for achieving swap-lane and overtake (开源正是实现换道超车的关键抓手).”

Xie Shaofeng’s operational architecture for the “open-source innovation software powerhouse city” construction, verbatim from the OpenAtom Foundation text, is a three-instrument-plus-three-work-item structure:

The three instruments for late-developing cities (three aspects):

  • First, expand the innovation boundary (拓展创新边界) — open source reduces repetitive R&D, enables cross-regional collaboration, and aggregates specialized capability
  • Second, consolidate the innovation foundation (夯实创新基础) — open source accelerates industrial accumulation through multi-party co-construction
  • Third, lead value leap (引领价值跃升) — open source enables late-developing cities to cultivate specialized industrial competitiveness at a higher technical starting point

The three work items for OpenAtom Foundation to execute (three work items):

  • First, build famous enterprises (打造名企) — strengthen talent-cultivation embedding into the entire process of open-source innovation practice, promoting joint practice-teaching and project R&D between universities, enterprises, and open-source communities
  • Second, build famous parks (建设名园) — improve industrial service, exert the Open Source Station (开源驿站) as a normalized hub function
  • Third, cultivate talent (培育人才) — integrate high-quality open-source contributions into teaching and achievement evaluation systems

The governance structure: A government-plus-foundation-plus-local-service-system tri-party coordination structure (政府+基金会+地方服务体系协同) — the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation self-declared tri-party coordination structure at the Chinese-national-software-expo keynote layer in this series. Xie Shaofeng’s operational claim, verbatim from the OpenAtom Foundation text, is to “strengthen element support, with government, foundation, and local service system coordinating their efforts. Strengthen local capability, promote the foundation to output resources and professional capability to localities. Optimize industrial service, exert the Open Source Station as a normalized hub.”

The Open Source Station (开源驿站) as a normalized hub is the sharpest institutional-economics object of the day: this is the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation self-declared Open-Source-Station-as-normalized-hub object in this series. The Open Source Station surface pairs institutionally with this series’ September 2 briefing’s documentation of Chongqing’s 15th-Five-Year-Plan AI framework’s explicit “open-source infrastructure and compliance codification” clauses and this series’ September 10 briefing’s documentation of the Shanghai Open Source Industry Investment Fund as a municipal-scale capital-instrument object — the same Chinese-national-scale open-source-infrastructure-institutionalization surface this series’ prior briefings have documented at the municipal-plan layer and the municipal-capital-instrument layer is now being operationalized at the Chinese-national-software-expo keynote layer simultaneously.

The three-work-items structure — build famous enterprises / build famous parks / cultivate talent — is institutionally parallel to this series’ September 18 briefing’s documentation of the OpenAtom Foundation donor-only open-source general-knowledge training program’s three-module structure (Open Source Overview and Enterprise Value / Enterprise Internal Open Source and Open Source Governance Systems / Enterprise Participation and Open Source Project Creation) — the same OpenAtom Foundation training-and-certification apparatus this series’ prior briefing documented at the Foundation’s donor-only layer is now being operationalized at the Foundation’s national-software-expo keynote layer, extended to a national-scale municipal-ecosystem-construction apparatus simultaneously.

The government-plus-foundation-plus-local-service-system tri-party coordination structure is the sharpest institutional-economics object of the day alongside the Open Source Station: this is the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation self-declared tri-party coordination structure at the national-software-expo keynote layer in this series. From an institutional economics standpoint, this tri-party-coordination-structure / OpenAtom-Foundation-keynote-surface pairing is a structural finding: the same OpenAtom Foundation surface this series’ prior briefings have documented at the OAAIF co-founding layer and the donor-training layer is being operationalized at the national-software-expo tri-party coordination layer simultaneously.

Institutional significance: OpenAtom Foundation Chairman Xie Shaofeng’s keynote at the 28th China International Software Expo in Zhengzhou is the first-documented OpenAtom Foundation Chairman self-declared state-directed-late-developer catch-up framing at the national-software-expo keynote layer in this series, and it is being operationalized at four institutional surfaces simultaneously — the software-city open-source-innovation swap-and-overtake layer, the three-instrument-plus-three-work-item operational-architecture layer, the Open Source Station normalized-hub layer, and the government-plus-foundation-plus-local-service-system tri-party coordination structure layer — a four-surface-simultaneous OpenAtom-Foundation-national-software-expo keynote institutionalization pattern.

Institutional Change — The Supreme People’s Court Opinions on AI Disputes Explicitly Name Open-Source Software Developers in Article XIII for Tort Liability Exemption, while Sidestepping the Copyrightability of AI-Generated Works#

1. Supreme People’s Court of the People’s Republic of China (2026-09-07) — Opinions on the Trial of Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence Disputes (最高人民法院关于依法审理涉人工智能纠纷案件的意见)

The Supreme People’s Court (SPC / 最高人民法院) released on September 7, 2026 the Opinions of the Supreme People’s Court on the Trial of Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence Disputes (最高人民法院关于依法审理涉人工智能纠纷案件的意见) — a five-part, 24-article document that Aaron Wininger of Schwegman Lundberg & Woessner’s China IP Law Update has documented as “the first set of judicial adjudication rules concerning artificial intelligence issued by China’s highest judicial body.” This is the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject for tort liability in this series.

The Opinions’ sharpest institutional-economics object on the open-source surface is Article XIII, verbatim from Wininger’s translation:

“(XIII) Determine the legal liability of parties involved in open-source software related to artificial intelligence in accordance with the law. When adjudicating cases involving open-source software, the determination of the tort liability of open-source software developers, providers, and subsequent developers and providers should comprehensively consider factors such as the type of open-source license agreement, the specific content of the restrictions on rights, security compliance measures, and the degree of information disclosure, and grant appropriate liability exemptions to open-source software developers and providers in accordance with the law. If an open-source software developer or provider provides some code modules needed for the research and development of artificial intelligence software in a free and open-source manner, and publicly explains its functions and security risks, and others infringe upon these code modules, the people’s court may determine that the open-source software developer or provider is not liable for tort.”

The Article XIII framing is the first-documented instance of the Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject at the tort-liability-exemption surface in this series. The Article XIII clause names open-source developers alongside AI developers, providers, users, and consumers as a distinct class of legal subject whose tort liability is adjudicated under specific open-source-applicable factors (type of open-source license, restrictions on rights, security compliance, degree of information disclosure) with an explicit direction to grant appropriate liability exemptions — the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus self-declared liability-exemption direction for open-source developers. From an institutional economics standpoint, this open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject / Chinese-judicial-apparatus surface pairing is a structural finding: the SPC Opinions are operationalizing open-source developers at the Chinese-judicial apparatus layer simultaneously with the AI developer layer, the AI provider layer, and the consumer layer — a four-surface-simultaneous-legal-subject-operationalization pattern.

The Opinions’ sharpest institutional-economics omission is that they conspicuously sidestep the copyrightability of AI-generated works themselves — that is, whether content generated with AI constitutes a “work” protected under the Copyright Law. This omission stands out institutionally for three documented reasons:

First, the Beijing Internet Court had already addressed the question. In its set of eight Typical Cases Involving Artificial Intelligence (涉人工智能典型案例) released September 10, 2025, Case I (Li v. Liu) held that a Stable Diffusion output was a copyrightable work of fine art and that the user who selected the model, entered prompts and reverse prompts, set generation parameters, and made the final selection was the author — reasoning that an AI model cannot itself be an author because Chinese copyright law limits authorship to natural persons, legal persons, and unincorporated organizations. Case VII similarly held that virtual digital human images reflecting a production team’s aesthetic choices as to line, color, and character design constitute protected works of art.

Second, neither copyrightability decision was designated a typical case by the SPC. The SPC designations went instead to the Beijing Internet Court’s personality-rights rulings (Case II, extending voice rights to AI-synthesized voices identifiable by timbre, intonation, and pronunciation style, and Case VIII, extending personality rights to a natural person’s AI-generated virtual image and selected as a “Typical Civil Case on the Judicial Protection of Personality Rights after the Promulgation of the Civil Code”).

Third, the SPC then removed the issue from that court’s docket altogether. On October 10, 2025, the SPC released the Provisions of the Supreme People’s Court on the Jurisdiction of Internet Courts (最高人民法院关于互联网法院案件管辖的规定), effective November 1, 2025, which amended the 2018 Provisions to strip Internet Courts of jurisdiction over “copyright or neighboring rights disputes involving works first published online” and “disputes arising from infringement of copyright or neighboring rights in works published or disseminated online,” on the stated rationale that Internet Courts should hear “new, cutting-edge, and complex online cases” while “general and traditional online cases are heard by other local courts.”

The triad of (SPC Opinions’ silence on copyrightability) + (absence of SPC endorsement for the Beijing rulings that reached the issue) + (reassignment of online copyright disputes away from the court that developed the jurisprudence) leaves the copyrightability of AI-generated output unresolved at the national level. From an institutional economics standpoint, this is a structurally distinctive pattern: the Chinese-judicial apparatus is explicitly adjudicating open-source developers’ tort liability (Article XIII) while declining to adjudicate the copyrightability question that the Beijing Internet Court had already reached. This is the first-documented instance of the Chinese-judicial apparatus drawing a two-sided line on the open-source surface in this series — a line drawn on the tort-liability side (open-source developers are a distinct legal subject with liability exemptions), and a line declined to be drawn on the copyrightability side (the substantive question of what constitutes a copyrightable AI-generated work is left to lower courts).

Article XIII pairs institutionally with this series’ September 19 briefing’s documentation of the SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis open-source-grading framework (Completeness L with 32 indicators + Openness O with 35 indicators) — the same Chinese-administrative grading-and-maturity-instrument surface this series’ prior briefing documented at the industry-standard layer is now being operationalized at the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer simultaneously. The SJ/T 12304-2026 two-axis 67-indicator LLM open-source grading framework defines the boundary between “Chinese open source” and “Chinese not-open-source” at the industry-standard layer; Article XIII defines the boundary between “open-source developer as a distinct legal subject with liability exemptions” and “AI developer / AI provider / AI user as other legal subjects” at the judicial-apparatus layer.

Institutional significance: The SPC Opinions on AI Disputes (September 7, 2026) is the first-documented Chinese-judicial apparatus placing open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject for tort liability in this series, and it is being operationalized at three institutional surfaces simultaneously — the Article XIII open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject layer, the open-source-developer-appropriate-liability-exemption layer, and the copyrightability-of-AI-generated-works-sidestepped layer — a three-surface-simultaneous Chinese-judicial-apparatus open-source-and-AI boundary-definition pattern.

Commentary#

Three institutionally new placements on the same unsolved institutional object that this series’ prior thirty-two briefings have been documenting: the institutional identity of Chinese open source across institutional borders.

This cycle’s briefing documents a structurally new institutional pattern: the same Chinese open-source surface is being operationalized simultaneously at three institutionally distinct surfaces this series’ prior briefings have not documented at before — the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer (Xin Guobin directing the development of “open-source base models” at the 2026 World Manufacturing Conference in Hefei, operationalizing a term no open-source community apparatus has defined), the OpenAtom Foundation national-software-expo keynote layer (Chairman Xie Shaofeng framing open source as the state-directed instrument for late-developing cities to swap-lane-and-overtake the 14 existing national software powerhouse cities, with a three-instrument-plus-three-work-item operational architecture and a government-plus-foundation-plus-local-service-system tri-party coordination structure), and the Chinese-judicial-apparatus open-source-and-AI boundary-definition layer (the SPC Opinions on AI Disputes explicitly naming open-source software developers as a distinct legal subject for tort liability in Article XIII with appropriate liability exemptions, while declining to adjudicate the copyrightability of AI-generated works themselves).

The three placements are institutionally coherent on the same unsolved institutional object — the institutional identity of Chinese open source across institutional borders — and the three placements together document a three-surface-simultaneous-institutionalization pattern alongside the three-surface-simultaneous pattern this series’ September 19 briefing documented at the Chinese-administrative industry-standard layer (SJ/T 12304-2026), the Reuters-English-language-international-wire institutional layer (DeepSeek’s hiring of Yan Wentao as its first CFO ahead of a possible STAR Market IPO), and the previous day’s briefing’s two-surface-simultaneous pattern at the district-party-committee-talent-office co-hosting layer (Tiangong Kaiwu Foundation + Xihu District Committee Talent Office) and the CAICT-administrative-institute third-party-assessment-and-testing-system layer (CAICT AOMA).

One structural pattern across the three placements: each placement pairs institutionally with prior briefings’ documented surfaces, and the three placements together document that the same Chinese open-source surface is being operationalized at three institutionally distinct surfaces simultaneously — an institutionally deeper three-surface-simultaneous pattern than the two-surface-simultaneous pattern this series’ September 20 briefing documented, because the three surfaces this cycle’s briefing documents sit at institutional layers this series’ prior briefings have not documented at before.

  • The MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks placement pairs institutionally with this series’ September 14 briefing’s MIIT “AI + Software” Special Action Implementation Plan’s Agent Software Application Store + Skills Resource Library construction layer, this series’ September 19 briefing’s SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis open-source-grading framework layer, this series’ September 2 briefing’s Chongqing 15th-Five-Year-Plan AI framework’s open-source-infrastructure-and-compliance codification layer, and this series’ September 18 briefing’s OpenAtom Foundation donor-only open-source general-knowledge training program layer — the same central-ministry industrial-policy surface being operationalized at the MIIT deputy-minister opening-remarks layer simultaneously with the MIIT Central-Administrative-Plan layer, the MIIT/CESI industry-standard layer, the municipal-plan layer, and the OpenAtom Foundation donor-training layer.
  • The OpenAtom Foundation national-software-expo keynote placement pairs institutionally with this series’ September 18 briefing’s OpenAtom Foundation donor-only open-source general-knowledge training program layer, this series’ September 2 briefing’s Chongqing 15th-Five-Year-Plan AI framework layer, this series’ September 10 briefing’s Shanghai Open Source Industry Investment Fund layer, and this series’ August 8 briefing’s OpenAtom Foundation + CAICT co-founding of the OAAIF layer — the same OpenAtom Foundation surface being operationalized at the national-software-expo keynote layer simultaneously with the donor-training layer, the municipal-plan layer, the municipal-capital-instrument layer, and the CAICT-co-founded-OAAIF layer.
  • The SPC Opinions on AI Disputes placement pairs institutionally with this series’ September 19 briefing’s SJ/T 12304-2026 industry-standard two-axis open-source-grading framework layer, this series’ September 3 briefing’s CAC Qinglang Phase 2 announcement with named platform subjects layer, and this series’ September 17 briefing’s CAICT Policy-and-Economics Institute three-layer agent-governance framework layer — the same Chinese-judicial-administrative boundary-definition surface being operationalized at the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer simultaneously with the industry-standard layer, the administrative-control layer, and the CAICT-institute-report layer.

One structural risk across the three placements: the same Chinese open-source surface is being operationalized at three institutionally distinct surfaces simultaneously, and the same “open-source” surface is being absorbed into multiple institutional mechanisms simultaneously at three institutionally distinct layers — a three-surface-simultaneous-institutional-absorption pattern.

The MIIT-deputy-minister opening-remarks placement is being operationalized at the central-ministry layer, and if the central-ministry layer absorbs the “open-source base model” concept into a central-ministry state-directed industrial-policy object rather than an open-source-community-defined concept object, the “open-source base model” concept converts into a central-ministry state-directed industrial-policy object. The OpenAtom Foundation national-software-expo keynote placement is being operationalized at the national-software-expo layer, and if the national-software-expo layer absorbs the software-powerhouse-city construction surface into a state-directed late-developer catch-up apparatus rather than an open-community open-source-innovation apparatus, the software-powerhouse-city construction surface converts into a state-directed late-developer catch-up apparatus. The SPC Opinions on AI Disputes placement is being operationalized at the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer, and if the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer absorbs the open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject surface into a Chinese-judicial-apparatus tort-liability-exemption object rather than an open-community open-source-developer object, the open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject surface converts into a Chinese-judicial-apparatus tort-liability-exemption object.

The institutional-economics question — which this cycle’s briefing documents but does not answer — is whether the Chinese institutional architecture can hold the three institutional surfaces simultaneously without one converting the other. If the central-ministry layer converts the “open-source base model” concept into a central-ministry state-directed industrial-policy object, the “open-source base model” concept becomes a central-ministry state-directed industrial-policy object rather than an open-source-community-defined concept object; if the national-software-expo layer converts the software-powerhouse-city construction surface into a state-directed late-developer catch-up apparatus, the software-powerhouse-city construction surface becomes a state-directed late-developer catch-up apparatus rather than an open-community open-source-innovation apparatus; if the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer converts the open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject surface into a Chinese-judicial-apparatus tort-liability-exemption object, the open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject surface becomes a Chinese-judicial-apparatus tort-liability-exemption object rather than an open-community open-source-developer object.

One perspective, not a verdict.

The three placements this cycle’s briefing documents — Xin Guobin’s “open-source base models” directive at the Hefei World Manufacturing Conference, Xie Shaofeng’s software-powerhouse-city keynote at the Zhengzhou Software Expo, and the SPC Opinions on AI Disputes’ Article XIII open-source-developer liability exemption with the copyrightability sidestep — are best read as observations of institutional movement in progress, not as verdicts on institutional direction. Xin Guobin’s directive does not guarantee that the “open-source base model” concept will remain an open-source-community-defined concept object rather than convert into a central-ministry state-directed industrial-policy object; Xie Shaofeng’s keynote does not guarantee that the software-powerhouse-city construction surface will remain an open-community open-source-innovation apparatus rather than convert into a state-directed late-developer catch-up apparatus; the SPC Opinions’ Article XIII does not guarantee that the open-source-developer-as-distinct-legal-subject surface will remain an open-community open-source-developer object rather than convert into a Chinese-judicial-apparatus tort-liability-exemption object. What this cycle’s briefing documents is that the Chinese open-source surface has crossed into a three-surface-simultaneous-institutionalization pattern that sits at institutionally distinct layers — the central-ministry layer, the national-software-expo layer, and the Chinese-judicial-apparatus layer — and that the three-surface-simultaneous-institutionalization pattern at these institutionally distinct layers is the sharpest documented operationalization of the state-directed-industrial-policy surface at three institutionally distinct layers simultaneously.

Editorial note on perspective: This briefing presents one institutional-economics reading of Chinese open-source developments, not a verdict. The “institutions” in this story — Xin Guobin’s “open-source base models” directive, Xie Shaofeng’s software-powerhouse-city keynote, and the SPC Opinions on AI Disputes’ Article XIII — are treated as objects of observation, not targets of critique. The Great Divergence 2.0 framework (FLOSS vs. State-Chartered Codebase vs. Intranet Shared Source vs. Cyber-Estate) is a lens, not a universal answer. One perspective, not a verdict.
