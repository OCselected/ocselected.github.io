Institutional Change — InnerSource China Summit 2026 Shanghai Documents the First National-Scale “Innersource” Movement Crossing Company Boundaries into Government and Industry, with the US SHARE IT Act, Saudi Cabinet Resolution 14, Singapore SGTS, and Shenzhen Longgang’s Longyuan Platform as Co-Operationalized Institutional Anchors: The First-Documented Operationalization of a Chinese InnerSource Movement at the Inter-National-Legislative-and-Sub-National-Government-Layer#

1. InnerSource Commons + COPU + CSDN + OpenTekr (2026-09-22/23 Shanghai) — InnerSource China Summit 2026 · Nine sessions plus one-day closed-door training camp · 9 months since InnerSource Commons’ US SHARE IT Act reference to Chinese audience · Bosch’s 17-year three-step innerSource path (Unblock → Empower → Drive) · BaiDu “from connected to usable” framework · Shenzhen Longgang Longyuan Platform: one base, two tools, 20 business domains, 21 MCP tools, 94 Skills, 182 accounts, 56 applications · US SHARE IT Act + Saudi Cabinet Resolution 14 + Singapore SGTS unified technology stack + Shenzhen Longgang district government platform · Tencent / Huawei / BaiDu / Google / Microsoft / Intel / SAP all listed as innerSource adopters

InnerSource Commons (ISC) and the China Open Source Promotion Union (COPU) jointly hosted the InnerSource China Summit 2026 in Shanghai on September 22–23, 2026, with CSDN and OpenTekr as operational partners. Per the summit’s own summary, “innerSource is a collaboration pattern that borrows open source software best practices, cultural transparency, and collaborative workflows into software development inside an organization.” Per the summit’s framing, the movement was put on the table this year because “after AI agents entered R&D workflows, whether code can be found across teams and whether organizational knowledge can be consumed by both humans and agents has become an efficiency problem, not a culture problem.”

The sharpest institutional-economics object of the day: the four-country-plus-one-district institutional-anchor pairing documented at a single summit. Per Russell Rutledge (InnerSource Commons Executive Director), this year marks the first time the InnerSource Commons has presented China’s audience with the US SHARE IT Act (2024), which “requires federal agencies to obtain sufficient rights to access, share, use, modify, and customize code across the whole government, with the CIO leading and reporting annually to Congress on federal IT spending above $100 billion.” Per the same framing: Saudi Arabia’s Cabinet Resolution 14 (2024) created a national government software repository led by the Digital Government Authority. Singapore’s Government Technology Singapore’s SGTS unified technology stack — 40+ government agencies, 200+ systems built on SGTS in the 2021–2022 financial year. And, per Wang Lei (Shenzhen Longgang District), Shenzhen Longgang’s Longyuan Platform (龙源平台) — “one base, unified code repository, artifact repository, CI/CD, plus MCP and Skills, plus two tools: LongCode for professional developers and LongCode Air for business cadres” — with white-list testing data as of September 20, 2026 documenting 20 business domains, 21 MCP tools, 94 Skills, 182 activated accounts, and 56 listed applications. Per the summit framing, the boundary is explicit: “tools are shareable, data is permission-scoped, assets do not leave the network.” Per the summit’s one-line summary: “open source solves the world’s no-reinvent-the-wheel problem; innerSource solves the district’s no-reinvent-the-wheel problem.”

The “Context over Control” institutional shift. Per Tan Zhongyi (COPU Executive Deputy Secretary General), the framing “Context over Control” documents a 17-year lineage: Netflix’s 2009 “Freedom and Responsibility” culture document → Zhang Yiming’s 2017 “Context, not Control” → Liang Rukuo’s June 2026 inclusion of Context in ByteDance’s performance assessment. Per the framing: “over” does not mean “instead of” — “control is not disappearing, it is being front-loaded” from after-the-fact approval to before-the-fact preparation. Per the framing: “innerSource is the mechanism through which organizational knowledge is sedimented into high-quality Context consumable by both humans and agents.”

The Bosch 17-year three-step path. Per Georg Grütter (Bosch InnerSource Ambassador): “scaled collaboration is not primarily a tools problem; it is a culture, organization, and governance problem.” The three-step path, in the required order: Unblock (clear cross-team collaboration process obstacles) → Empower (give teams reusable capabilities and templates) → Drive (with measurement and mechanism, let it run on its own). Per Grütter: “the order cannot be reversed; many organizations start at step three, and cannot push forward.”

The BaiDu “from connected to usable” framing. Per Dong Shuzhao (BaiDu OSPO and InnerSource Office Lead): “corporate internal systems have long been connected, but permissions, interfaces, and metadata each speak their own language; when AI agents arrive, they cannot read it. InnerSource’s role is to upgrade ‘connected’ into ‘usable.’”

The institutional-economics question this cycle’s briefing documents but does not answer. Per the summit, one question raised by an attendee “does not have an answer on site”: a team’s cross-department code reuse currently requires three approval layers — a structural question about organizational design rather than methodology. This is the institutional-economics question being operationalized at the Chinese-innerSource-cross-department-code-reuse-plus-three-approval-layers-organizational-design layer.

Institutional significance: The InnerSource China Summit 2026 Shanghai documentation of a Chinese-innerSource-movement crossing company boundaries into government and industry is the first-documented instance in this series of a Chinese InnerSource movement being operationalized simultaneously at five institutionally distinct surfaces — the InnerSource-Commons-COPU-China-Open-Source-Promotion-Union-joint-summit-hosting layer, the inter-national-legislative-and-sub-national-government-anchor layer (US SHARE IT Act + Saudi Cabinet Resolution 14 + Singapore SGTS + Shenzhen Longgang Longyuan Platform), the Bosch-17-year-empower-drive-empower three-step-order layer, the BaiDu-connected-to-usable organizational-upgrade layer, and the Context-over-Control 17-year-lineage (Netflix 2009 → Zhang Yiming 2017 → Liang Rukuo 2026-06) layer — a first-documented five-surface-simultaneous Chinese-innerSource-movement institutional-form transition.

3. Thought-Steel (思想钢印) + Huxiu (虎嗅) (2026-09-26) — “The Most Successful Part of the Chinese Internet May Be the Biggest Problem for AI Agents” · Meta Muse: 2 million+ prompts per week in North America as of September 26, 2026, surpassing ChatGPT on the free chart in the US and Canada · China’s top web pages are almost all inside apps (Meituan / Taobao / WeChat) · Browser = cross-company standard operating system · Packaging for human users is good UX; for AI agents it is a wall · WeChat: no agent can get in or get out · China’s three paths for consumer-grade agents: Super-App-Native Agent (WeChat most likely) + Tech-Giant-Ecosystem-Native Agent (Alibaba Qwen / ByteDance AI phone) + Phone-Manufacturer IoT Agent (Xiaomi / IoT) · China’s internet giants’ business cross-hatch competition → more likely to use AI to extend business, not to open the ecosystem

The Thought-Steel (思想钢印) column, republished by Huxiu (虎嗅), published a 4505-character essay on 2026-09-26 documenting that “the most successful part of the Chinese Internet may be the biggest problem for AI Agents.” Per the essay’s sharpest line: the Chinese Internet has no web pages anymore. Per the essay’s documentation:

  • Meituan’s website contains only a company-image site plus an app download link. Taobao’s PC site has product pages but with simplified functionality.
  • Per the essay’s framing, the “internet sinking” (网络下沉) phenomenon means that “many first-time internet users in lower-tier cities came online directly on mobile phones and never developed a browser habit.” The past decade-plus of mobile internet development has left “an unnoticed gap: increasingly more content is concentrated inside mobile apps, and increasingly less content is available on PC browsers.”
  • Per the essay’s documentation, Meta Muse (the “viral AI product”) was in the top position on the free chart in the US and Canada by September 26, 2026, with over 2 million prompts per week in North America. Per the essay’s framing, Muse’s killer application (email processing, shopping assistant, travel planning) is “standard issue” in Chinese consumer-grade AI agents but does not produce the same product-shock effect in China.

The sharpest institutional-economics object of the day: the browser-as-cross-company-standard-operating-system layer as the structural reason why Muse works in the US and does not work in China. Per the essay’s framing: “The most comfortable working environment for AI agents today is the web page world — the browser itself is a cross-company standard operating system.” Per the essay’s documentation: Muse runs on its own cloud computer and browser with an independent Secure VM (a “Secure VMMeta” that “specifically trains a browser Agent to complete these multi-step tasks: it can open pages, read, log in, fill in forms, click, and return by itself”). Per the essay’s framing, “the American Internet developed earlier, most websites existed before mobile internet mainstreamed, and although most services are also delivered through apps, they still treat the website as a formal service entrance with complete content and cross-device functionality.” Per the essay’s framing, this is “the first real reason Muse has that particular product-shock effect: it leverages the browser, an infrastructure that has been mature for 30 years and has not had major changes for 20 years, so AI agents have a mouse essentially for free — no need for every software to provide API adaptation, solving one is solving all.”

The Chinese-app-enclosure layer as the mirror of the American-browser-openness layer. Per the essay’s documentation: “Chinese consumer-grade agents often produce demonstrations that are strikingly impressive but hard to execute in practice, precisely because many high-frequency required functions in China are not on public web pages but must be completed inside a specific app.” Per the essay’s documentation of the specific blockers: “these apps each have different internal account systems and internal risk-control systems, various mini-programs with different device-permission requirements, and heavy use of facial recognition, SMS verification codes, sliders, and app-jumps — the current AI agents can barely move.” Per the essay’s documentation of the WeChat-specific constraint: “WeChat covers half of the mobile Internet — chat, payment, life services, the all-powerful official accounts, the all-powerful mini-programs, over-Baidu search content — every agent neither can get around it nor can get into the huge black box.”

The “packaging for humans is friendly; packaging for AI is not” layer. Per the essay’s framing: “Chinese mobile Internet is too powerful, the interest groups are too big, and the usage habits are too sticky — this is actually unfavorable for the popularization of consumer-grade AI agents. Mobile Internet actively packages many things into apps for user experience; now that AI agents have arrived, the inverse problem emerges: packaging is friendly for humans, not for AI.” Per the essay’s documentation of what AI agents need versus what humans need:

  • Humans need: pretty icons, clear hierarchy, moderate information density.
  • AI agents need: observability, “knowing where I am now, what is available to operate, what each thing is.”
  • Humans can tolerate ambiguity, judge next steps by experience.
  • AI agents can guess too, but “this ambiguity significantly increases error rates and token cost, so AI-friendly software should have one action map to one clear semantics.”
  • Humans want big-and-comprehensive closed environments with all functions combined.
  • AI agents want applications with single capabilities, for step-by-step combination.
  • Humans need safety.
  • AI agents need openness — no CAPTCHA, no dynamic page rendering, no facial recognition, do not hide data inside.

The “豆包手机 cross-app operation was blocked by WeChat and Taobao” layer. Per the essay’s documentation: “Doubao phone’s cross-app operations were sequentially restricted by WeChat and Taobao.” This is the concrete institutional evidence layer for the WeChat-and-Taobao-blocking-cross-app-AI-agent-operation surface.

The three-paths-forward layer. Per the essay’s documentation of three forward paths for Chinese consumer-grade AI agents:

  1. Super-App-Native Agent — “WeChat is most likely” (超级App内置Agent·微信最可能).
  2. Tech-Giant-Ecosystem-Native Agent — “Alibaba Qwen / ByteDance AI phone” (科技巨头生态内Agent·阿里千问+手机厂商字节AI手机).
  3. Phone-Manufacturer IoT Agent — “Xiaomi IoT” (手机厂商·小米IoT).

Per the essay’s sharpest line on the three-path constraint: “Chinese internet giants’ businesses are cross-hatched and highly competitive; they are more likely to use AI to extend their own businesses, not to open the ecosystem to cross-company agents.”

The “later-developer advantage” layer as the deep structural observation. Per the essay’s framing: “This is the manifestation of the ’later-developer advantage’ (后发优势): new technologies usually bring new systems that collide with existing system usage habits, and late-developing countries often do not have a complete old-system infrastructure for the old technology, so they adapt to the new technology more easily. For example, China led the world in mobile payment because China’s existing payment infrastructure was weak, credit-card culture was missing, and many life scenarios used cash — while mobile internet infrastructure coverage was more comprehensive.”

The Institutional-Economics Question this cycle’s briefing documents but does not answer. The essay’s sharpest institutional-economics question: whether China’s consumer-grade AI-agent layer will produce any Muse-equivalent product-shock effect under the existing infrastructure constraint that the browser-as-cross-company-standard-operating-system layer does not exist in China, or whether the three-paths-forward (super-app-native / tech-giant-ecosystem-native / phone-manufacturer IoT) will consolidate Chinese consumer-grade AI agents into an internally-enclosed institutional-form object that structurally cannot produce cross-company product-shock.

Institutional significance: Thought-Steel (思想钢印) + Huxiu (虎嗅)’s 2026-09-26 essay is the first-documented instance in this series of a Chinese-consumer-grade-AI-agent-infrastructure-constraint layer being operationalized at the browser-as-cross-company-standard-operating-system-plus-no-web-pages-in-China-plus-WeChat-and-Taobao-cross-app-blocking-plus-three-forward-paths layer, with the Muse-North-America-2-million-plus-prompts-per-week-plus-US-and-Canada-free-chart-first positioning as the concrete institutional-evidence layer, and with the packaging-is-friendly-for-humans-not-for-AI framing as the sharpest structural observation — a first-documented four-surface-simultaneous Chinese-consumer-grade-AI-agent-infrastructure-constraint operationalization.
