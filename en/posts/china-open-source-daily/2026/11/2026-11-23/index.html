China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-23

One licence text settles this morning. On 2026-11-20 Tencent released the Hy4 flagship — 770B total parameters, 49B active, a one-million-token context — under a document titled the Tencent Hy License, Version 1.0, which opens by saying it is derived from Apache 2.0, is not Apache 2.0, and is not endorsed by the Apache Software Foundation. Section 10 then adds the term that matters: anyone operating a model-as-a-service business with more than USD 50 million of worldwide gross revenue in any twelve-month window, or more than 100 million monthly active users, must obtain a separate written licence from Tencent before using the model or any derivative of it — and the definition of derivative explicitly names fine-tuning, quantisation, distillation, merging and pruning. Hy4 preview, released 28 August, shipped under genuine Apache 2.0 with no use restrictions and is still tagged apache-2.0 on Hugging Face this morning. The same family, the same vendor, two entirely different institutional instruments, four months apart. The repository that carries the new licence was created 2026-10-28, holds five commits, all from one account, and no model weights at all — models live on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode. One more document from the same weekend is the fourth Chinese vendor in this series to attach its own name to a claimed national installation base: 24 million systems, 8,800 ecosystem partners, 160,000 adapted applications. The licence is the day's evidence. The repository is the day's second evidence, and it is quieter.

⚠️ Editorial note: The open source ecosystem in China operates under a distinct institutional framework — characterized by state-led initiatives, intranet-like boundaries, and top-down governance. Readers should be aware that this context differs from the community-driven open source model common in other regions. The term “open source” as used in Chinese media may refer to practices that diverge from the conventional definition.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-23#

⚖️ Legal & Licensing — The Preview Was the Apache Licence: What Section 10 of the Tencent Hy License Actually Collects#

1. The announcement, in the wording the vendor used. On 2026-11-20, the Tencent Hy (混元) team announced that Hy4 was open-sourced, with weights published simultaneously on GitHub, Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode. The model: a mixture-of-experts architecture, 770B total parameters, 49B activated per token (plus 1.23B vision), a 1,048,576-token context, a 5-layer DFly2 speculative drafter of 3.3B, and Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention with IndexCache — the README says plainly that the attention design was “inspired by DeepSeek and GLM.” Alongside the flagship, Hy4-FP8 and the pre-trained Hy4 base were released, plus a community GGUF conversion. The IT Home account of the same morning states the licence in one sentence: “Hy4 and Hy4 preview use the Apache 2.0 licence, permitting commercial use and modification.” That sentence is the one this briefing was written to check, and it does not survive contact with the file.

2. What the file says, because the file is short. The LICENSE in github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy4 — 245 lines, retrieved this morning — opens: “Tencent Hy License, Version 1.0. This License is derived from the Apache License, Version 2.0, with modifications, mainly the additional commercial terms for large Model-as-a-Service (‘MaaS’) businesses in Section 10. This License is not the Apache License, Version 2.0, and is not endorsed by or affiliated with the Apache Software Foundation.” The Hugging Face model card agrees and is machine-readable: tencent/Hy4 is tagged license:other with license_name: tencent-hy-license and a pointer back to the same file. The register is unambiguous: the vendor’s own artefact says it is not Apache 2.0. Only the press coverage says it is.

3. Section 10, in full, because the arithmetic is the argument. 10.1 defines MaaS as “giving third parties remote access to model inference or fine-tuning (e.g., via API), allowing them to submit inputs or specify parameters or training data”, excluding end-user products whose model capability is confined to specific features and excluding mere relaying to an independent third-party model service. 10.2, titled “Separate License Required”, overrides the copyright and patent grants of Sections 2 and 3: a party operating a MaaS business must obtain a separate written licence from Tencent (including by email) in either of two cases — (a) if its aggregate worldwide gross revenue exceeds USD 50 million in any consecutive twelve-month period, “whether or not such revenue is derived from the Work or any Derivative Works”; or (b) before using the Work or any Derivative in a product or service with more than 100 million monthly active users in any calendar month. 10.3 exempts purely internal use where neither the model, its derivatives, nor its outputs reach third parties. 10.4 gives the address: hy_partners@tencent.com.

4. The definition that widens the gate, and it is the sentence to keep. Section 1 defines Derivative Works and adds, for the avoidance of doubt: “Derivative Works include any fine-tuned, quantized, distilled, merged, pruned, or otherwise modified versions of the model weights.” This matters more than the revenue threshold, because it means the gate is not on the weights — it is on every competent downstream artefact built from them. A 770-billion-parameter model cannot be served casually by a small operator; the population that can productively use it consists of organisations that quantise it (FP8 is published by Tencent itself precisely to make it servable), fine-tune it for a domain, or distil it into something affordable. Section 10 attaches to exactly that population once it starts reselling inference. And note what the exemption is not: 10.3 protects the firm that uses Hy4 internally and ships nothing — the enterprise customer, the research lab. The obligation lands on the firm that turns the model into a service other people pay for. On the economics of a commons, that is a liability rule attached to the moment a value-added layer is created, which is the one layer a bare permissive licence cannot charge for.

5. The comparison the vendor already made, four months earlier. Hy4 preview was released 2026-08-28 under a genuine Apache 2.0 — its LICENSE file is the unmodified Apache text with Tencent’s copyright line above it (“Tencent Hy4 preview is licensed under the Apache-2.0”), and the Hugging Face card is still tagged license:apache-2.0 with no use restrictions at all. The switch happened at the frontier tier, not at the small-model tier: Hy3 preview, Hy3 and Hy4 preview went out under Apache 2.0; Hy4, Hy4-FP8 and Hy4 base went out under the Tencent Hy License. The thresholds are also structurally different from the licences this series examined on 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20, and the difference is diagnostic. Moonshot’s K3 licence gates on revenue generated from K3-related services. This one gates on aggregate worldwide gross revenue, whether or not such revenue is derived from the Work — a total-firm attribute, not a project attribute. The first is a royalty on a specific commons. The second is an access permit indexed to the size of the applicant. A permit indexed to size is not a price; it is a filter on who is allowed to resell the commons, and the filter favours the integrated firm over the independent one.

6. Where the weights actually live, because that is where the measurement is. The GitHub repository that carries the licence has no weights in it — its contents are LICENSE (13,942 bytes), README.md, README_CN.md, .gitignore, assets/ and finetune/. The 770B model is published as files on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode, with the licensing consequence that the terms of use live on a platform page and in a downloadable text file, not in a history anyone can audit. The GitHub side measures as: repository created 2026-10-28, five commits in total (2026-10-29, two on 2026-11-19, two on 2026-11-20), all five authored by a single account (yqc01), one contributor total, 73 stars, 3 forks, and GitHub’s licence detector returning NOASSERTION — its way of saying the licence is neither recognised nor standard. The two predecessors are the same shape: Hy3 (created 2026-07-02, three contributing accounts, 677 stars, 206 forks) and Hy4 preview (created 2026-08-27, two accounts, 452 stars, 31 forks) — every one of them a README-and-finetune-scripts repository with no weights and no outside patch. Three releases, three press cycles, and zero participation surfaces. A repository whose entire commit history is one employee editing README files is a distribution channel wearing a repository’s clothes — and every governance question a repository answers (who may merge, who reviews, whose patch counts) is unanswered because those questions have no addressee.

Institutional Analysis & Commentary:

This is the cleanest licensing specimen of the year, and the reason is that nothing here is hidden and nothing here is contested. The vendor published the terms, the platform re-tagged them, and the press summarised them wrongly. The error is instructive.

First, the substance, and it should be credited before it is measured. Tencent released 770 billion parameters of BF16 weights for its flagship model, including the pre-trained base and an FP8 quantisation, under a licence that permits unrestricted commercial use and modification for the overwhelming majority of users. The exemption in 10.3 exempts internal enterprise use entirely; the gate in 10.2 catches firms above USD 50 million in worldwide revenue and operating a model-as-a-service business. Most Chinese open-weight releases this year did the permissive thing — DeepSeek V4 and R1 under MIT, GLM-4.6 and GLM-5.2 under MIT, Qwen3-Coder-480B and Qwen3.8-27B under Apache 2.0 — and publishing weights at all, at this scale, is a real transfer of a real asset into a space firms cannot fully control. On Williamson’s L4 that is a genuine reduction in asset specificity for everyone downstream. The criticism that follows is not that Tencent did too little. It is that the institutional form chosen does something specific, and the form is being described as something else.

Second, the diagnosis. What is being sold here is not code but the right to resell a commons, and the licence solves that with a permit — and a permit issued at a threshold indexed to total firm revenue is a Coasean solution in the least Coasean way available. Coase’s 1960 result is that in the absence of transaction costs the assignment of rights affects distribution but not efficiency; the corollary every institutionalist draws is that the assignment therefore matters enormously wherever transaction costs are positive, and a permit to negotiate at the vendor’s discretion is exactly what it looks like when the right to negotiate is the entire instrument. Three consequences follow, and none is speculative. (a) The gate is not priced, so it is not a market mechanism. A firm above the threshold cannot compute the cost of compliance from the licence; it can only mail hy_partners@tencent.com. Compare the alternatives that cost nothing to administer and are fully legible: a royalty (Kimi’s 30 per cent ask, examined on 2026-11-20) or an automatic trigger with published terms (the Apache-2.0-plus-clause pattern several Chinese vendors now use). (b) The threshold is total revenue, so it prices the applicant, not the use. A USD 60 million systems integrator running a private inference service for three clients and a USD 5 billion hyperscaler pay the same procedural price: both must ask. (c) Section 10 attaches to derivatives — including quantisations — so it reaches the community layer that produces the affordable artefact. The FP8 release solves a memory problem for the vendor’s own serving stack; it does not grant anyone else the right to serve the quantisation commercially past the threshold. This is not a failure of openness. It is openness used as the entry ticket to a negotiation, and it is exactly the shape an asset owner adopts when it wants downstream adoption without downstream competition.

Third, and this is where the story is larger than one model. The repository measurement is the day’s real finding, because it is a pattern and not an anecdote. Three releases — Hy3, Hy4 preview, Hy4 — each with a press cycle, a licence page, and a commit history written entirely by one account, none of which received a patch from a non-Tencent contributor. The correct reading is not “Tencent is faking open source.” The correct reading is that the artefact being opened is the weights, and the weights were never in the repository. Chinese front-tier model releases have converged on a distribution architecture: permissive-or-conditional weights on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode for reach and for benchmarks; a GitHub organisation for the README, the licence, the inference snippets and the finetune scripts; and no contribution surface at all, because a repository whose every file is written by the releasing employee does not need one. This is a stable equilibrium for the vendor — adoption, citations and a Hub rank, with no merge right ceded. It is also a category confusion in the statistics that everyone, this series included, uses to discuss Chinese open source: “actively maintained repository,” “star growth,” “commit count” are measurements of participation that are here measuring publication. GitHub’s refusal to recognise the licence (NOASSERTION) is a small accidental honesty — the platform cannot name what is being released either.

Fourth, the fourth brand name, and the installation base that keeps being re-counted. On 2026-11-20, at the 2026 World Computing Conference (世界计算大会) in Changsha — hosted by the Hunan provincial government with the MIIT-affiliated CCID as co-organiser — Wang Guirong (王桂荣), vice-general-manager of China Electronics Corporation (中国电子, CEC), spoke under the title “Co-build and co-share, open and open-source: constructing a new ecology for the integrated development of the intelligent economy.” The shareable numbers: the PK system (Phytium CPU + Kylin OS) now reports 8,800-plus Phytium ecosystem partners, 160,000-plus adapted applications, 8.77 million entries in Kylin’s software-and-hardware ecosystem, and 24 million cumulative deployments; the platform is being reframed as an “AI-native operating system” positioned as the “agent of agents” (智能体之智能体), already compatible with more than ten third-party agents at the device level, with the KART device-cloud inference framework adapted to 16 GPUs and wrapping 90-plus operators; and in November 2026 CEC formally launched an “AI+” action building a 1+2+M+N system — one intelligent advanced foundation kit (compute chips, switching chips, AI-native OS, AI database, AI EDA), two platforms (converged intelligent compute, open intelligent information), internal group-wide AI support, and external scenarios. The argument for open source in the speech is unusually literal and worth quoting, because it is the sector’s standard justification: Linux’s thirty-plus years of open collaboration produced mechanisms of code sharing, community co-building and interface coordination; the kernel grew “from roughly 10,000 lines of code to more than 43 million lines today, contributed by tens of thousands of developers worldwide.” The same lineage is recited, from the opposite direction, by a different kind of institution, and the comparison is exact: OpenHarmony’s own reporting in June 2026 gives 140 million lines of community code, 13,000-plus contributors, 550-plus community partners, 51 donation partners, and — disclosed by its project-committee executive chair at OHDC 2026 — that “the share of non-Huawei developers, non-Huawei code contributions and non-Huawei committers continues to rise.” Both organisations claim the Linux model. One publishes the share of contributions that come from outside its founding company; the other publishes an installation base. That is the entire distance between an ecosystem and a distribution, and it is measurable from the two organisations’ own conference reporting.

Fifth, this run of the series is now four brands deep into one number, and the number should be retired until someone publishes a denominator. The claimed national installation bases accumulate: openEuler, 22 million systems (2026-11-13; examined on 2026-11-18 for its falling cost per installation); HarmonyOS devices above 100 million and OpenHarmony-ecosystem devices at 1.3 billion (June 2026); and 24 million cumulative deployments for PK (2026-11-20, above). Each is a cumulative, self-reported, vendor-branded count with no published contributor definition and no denominator. In the PK case one line of arithmetic deserves stating: 24 million deployments against 160,000 adapted applications is one application per 150 installations — a normal profile for a state-and-enterprise procurement base, where deployments arrive in institutional batches and applications arrive one certification at a time, and an abnormal one for a developer ecosystem, where adoption is usually application-first. The count is not false. It is a count of a procurement base being circulated as evidence of an ecosystem — and the institutions that can measure participation, by contribution share, are the ones not being quoted.

What to watch, concretely. Three checks will resolve within a quarter whether Section 10 is a routine legal backstop or the beginning of a licensing regime. One: whether Tencent publishes the terms it grants in response to hy_partners@tencent.com requests — an unpublished form contract is a discretionary permit, a published one is a licence. Two: whether github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy4 ever receives a commit from an account that is not yqc01; the record today is five commits, one account, one contributor, and a second person with merge rights would be the first governance surface in the family. Three: whether any downstream vendor publishes a fine-tuned or quantised Hy4 derivative under its own name — because under Section 1’s derivative definition that is precisely the act the gate was built to see, and the sector’s answer to it will define what “open weights” means in Chinese practice for the next eighteen months. On the day’s documents: the licence is legal, precise, drafted by competent counsel, and honourable. It is also not Apache 2.0 — and the gap between the document and its press reception is the most informative thing published in Chinese open source this weekend.
