Readers should be aware that this context differs from the community-driven open source model common in other regions. The term “open source” as used in Chinese media may refer to practices that diverge from the conventional definition.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-08-28#

🏛️ State Council Information Office Press Conference (August 26, 2026) — MIIT’s Liu Yulin Frames Open Source as a Three-Pillar Industrial-System Instrument, and Announces OpenHarmony at 1.35 Billion Devices as the World’s Third-Largest Mobile OS#

1. CCTV / State Council Information Office (SCIO) — 8月26日 “开局起步’十五五’” series press conference, MIIT Information-Communication-Development Department Director Liu Yulin (刘郁林); headline “强供给 促应用 育生态 多维度发力乘势而上用好新一代信息技术” (Strengthen Supply, Promote Application, Cultivate Ecosystem — Multi-Dimensional Mobilization to Leverage New-Generation Information Technology), republished on 央视网 (CCTV News) and 开放原子开源基金会 (OpenAtom Foundation), 2026-08-26.

On August 26, 2026, the State Council Information Office (国务院新闻办公室 / SCIO) hosted its “开局起步’十五五’” (Launch-of-the-15th-Five-Year-Plan) thematic press-conference series. The MIIT information-communication-development director 刘郁林 (Liu Yulin) delivered three institutional announcements in one session:

(a) The “Strong-Supply / Promote-Application / Cultivate-Ecosystem” three-pillar open-source strategy — 强供给 / 促应用 / 育生态. Liu Yulin framed China’s open-source program for the 15th Five-Year Plan period as a three-pillar industrial-system instrument: strong supply (加大研发投入, 创新组织方式 — increased R&D investment, new organizational modes, concentrated attacks on key technologies; 实施工业数据筑基行动 — industrial-data foundation action; 深入推进"人工智能+软件"行动 — deepening AI-plus-software action), promote application (鼓励各行各业大胆开放应用场景 — encourage all sectors to open application scenarios; 从"用软件"向"享服务"转变 — transition from “using software” to “enjoying services”), and cultivate ecosystem (鼓励开源社区和企业深化开放合作, 形成可持续商业模式 — encourage open-source communities and enterprises to deepen open cooperation, form sustainable business models).

(b) OpenHarmony ecosystem devices exceed 1.35 billion units; HarmonyOS phones are the world’s third-largest mobile operating system; 3,200 ecosystem partners; 14,000+ code contributors; sector-branded OS derivatives (工鸿 Gong-Hong for manufacturing, 仪鸿 Yi-Hong for instrumentation) deployed at scale across automobiles, home appliances, metallurgy, petrochemicals, and other sectors. The “13.5亿台” (1.35 billion) figure is MIIT’s official count of devices running an OpenHarmony-derived OS, and the 世界第三大 (world’s third-largest) ranking is MIIT’s own framing, not an independent-analyst count.

(c) AI open-source cumulative downloads exceed 10 billion globally; the national AI open-source community (AtomGit-anchored, as documented in prior briefings) aggregates 11 million+ users and hosts 2 million+ projects.

From an institutional economics standpoint, the SCIO press-conference event matters for five reasons:

First, it documents the first SCIO-tier formalization of a three-pillar open-source industrial strategy. Prior MIIT open-source documentation (assembled in this cycle’s August 15–21 briefings on the OpenAtom partner-expansion layer, talent-certification layer, and community-formation layer) operated at the industry-sector-policy layer — foundation governance documents, certification frameworks, partner-structure announcements. This cycle’s SCIO press conference operates at the state-council-policy-discourse layer — the SCIO is the State Council’s own press-dissemination arm, and the “十五五” series is the formal Five-Year-Plan launch-sequencing channel. From an institutional economics standpoint, this layer-upgrade is a structural finding: MIIT’s open-source industrial strategy has been elevated from the industry-sector-policy layer to the state-council-policy-discourse layer, and the three-pillar vocabulary (强供给 / 促应用 / 育生态) becomes the formal lexical shell for the 15th Five-Year Plan’s open-source chapter.

Second, it documents the OpenHarmony “1.35 billion devices” figure as the MIIT-authorized institutional count of the OpenHarmony ecosystem. The 13.5亿台 figure is MIIT’s own count, not an independent-analyst estimate. From an institutional economics standpoint, the MIIT-authorized-count / open-ecosystem-size pairing is a structural finding: OpenHarmony’s ecosystem size is now an MIIT-authorized institutional metric, and the metric is being disseminated through the SCIO channel rather than through Huawei’s own marketing or OpenHarmony Community’s own reporting. This shifts the metric-authority locus from ecosystem-internal to state-council-external.

Third, it documents the sector-branded-OS-derivative (工鸿 / 仪鸿 / 电鸿 Dian-Hong for power-grid, 车鸿 Che-Hong for automotive) architecture as MIIT’s institutional vocabulary for OpenHarmony’s cross-sector deployment. The 工鸿 / 仪鸿 / 电鸿 / 车鸿 naming pattern — “industry-character + Hong” — is the same Chinese-prefix naming-politics instrument documented in prior briefings on the 2026 China Linux Kernel Developer Conference’s China-prefix naming. From an institutional economics standpoint, the sector-branded-derivative / naming-politics pairing is a structural finding: OpenHarmony’s cross-sector deployment is not a single-OS rollout but a multi-derivative sector-specific architecture, and each derivative is institutionally branded with a Chinese industry-character prefix.

Fourth, it documents the AtomGit-anchored national AI open-source community as the institutional anchor of China’s AI open-source downloads. The 10 billion cumulative downloads figure is MIIT’s own count, and the 11 million users / 2 million projects figure is MIIT’s own count of the national AI open-source community. From an institutional economics standpoint, the AtomGit-anchored-community / MIIT-authorized-count pairing is a structural finding: AtomGit (the Huawei-initiated Chinese GitHub alternative, documented in prior briefings as one of the four links in the Great-Divergence-2.0 open-source-infrastructure chain) is now institutionally positioned as the national AI open-source community anchor, and its user-project-download counts are MIIT-authorized institutional metrics.

Fifth, it creates an institutional bridge between the three-pillar industrial-strategy layer and the SCIO-tier policy-discourse layer. Prior MIIT open-source institutional-expansion documentation operated on the industry-sector-policy layer. This cycle’s SCIO press conference operates on the state-council-policy-discourse layer. From an institutional economics standpoint, this two-layer institutional-architecture is a structural finding: MIIT’s open-source program now has a documented two-layer institutional-architecture — industry-sector-policy layer + state-council-policy-discourse layer — rather than a single-layer industry-sector-policy architecture.

🏛️ 2026 OpenAtom Open-Source Ecosystem Conference (June 25–26, 2026) — 198 Donors / 600 Million Yuan Cumulative Funding / Mulan License-Family 6-Protocol Donation / Yi-Hong and M-Robots Community Launches — The OpenAtom Foundation’s Two-Year-Cycle Institutional Snapshot#

2. 新华网 / Xinhua News (2026-06-25) and 科技日报 Science & Technology Daily (2026-06-26) coverage of the 2026 开放原子开源生态大会 (2026 OpenAtom Open-Source Ecosystem Conference), Beijing, June 25–26, 2026, theme “开源赋能产业，生态共筑未来” (Open Source Empowers Industry, Ecosystem Builds the Future).

The 2026 OpenAtom Open-Source Ecosystem Conference — the flagship annual event of the OpenAtom Foundation — convened on June 25–26, 2026, in Beijing. From an institutional economics standpoint, this cycle’s briefing documents five institutional announcements that together constitute the OpenAtom Foundation’s two-year-cycle institutional snapshot (the 2026 conference is the fourth edition since the foundation’s 2020 founding):

(a) Cumulative funding: 198 donors, over 600 million yuan cumulative fundraising since 2020. OpenAtom — the national-tier state-anchored open-source foundation, documented in prior briefings as the institutional anchor of China’s multi-foundation open-source architecture — reports 198 donating entities and over 600 million yuan cumulative fundraising since its 2020 founding. In 2026 to date: 9 new unrestricted donors joined, and 深开鸿 (ShenKaiHong / DeepKaiHong) and 拓维信息 (Talkweb Information) upgraded to platinum-tier donors — two companies that are themselves OpenHarmony ecosystem partners, making their platinum-tier upgrade a sector-reciprocal donor-ecosystem arrangement.

(b) Mulan license-family 6-protocol donation to the OpenAtom Foundation. The 2026 conference hosted the 木兰许可证族捐赠仪式 (Mulan License-Family Donation Ceremony), with six protocols covering distinct use-scenario categories. This is the first documented instance of a Chinese-characteristic open-source license family being formally donated to the OpenAtom Foundation, positioning OpenAtom as the institutional host of China’s bilingual-license governance architecture (as documented in Christopher K. Tong’s August 12 The Conversation article, which noted that MulanPSL 2.0 and the OpenAtom Model License 1.0 are both bilingual English-Chinese, with the Chinese version prevailing in cases of conflicting interpretation).

(c) 开放原子仪鸿开源社区 (OpenAtom Yi-Hong Instrumentation Open-Source Community) formally inaugurated. The Yi-Hong (仪鸿, “instrumentation + Hong”) community — the newest sector-branded derivative in OpenHarmony’s sector-branded-OS-derivative architecture (alongside Gong-Hong 工鸿 for manufacturing, Dian-Hong 电鸿 for power-grid, Che-Hong 车鸿 for automotive) — convened core members including 中国机械工业仪器仪表集团 (China Mechanical Industry Instrumentation Group), 中国华能 (China Huaneng), 中移物联网 (China Mobile IoT), and 拓维信息 (Talkweb) — spanning manufacturing, energy, IoT, and educational sectors.

(d) M-Robots (Robot Operating System) community formally launched; 龙蜥社区 (Anolis Linux Community) fully upgraded to an AI-native OS community; 龙架构社区 (LoongArch Community) and M-Robots launched to fill instruction-set and commercial-robotics open-source-ecosystem gaps.

(e) 源图 (YuanTu) — high-quality Skills dataset — released at the conference to address the domestic short-board in high-quality data supply for AI agents.

From an institutional economics standpoint, the 2026 OpenAtom conference matters for five reasons:

First, it documents the OpenAtom Foundation’s 198-donor / 600-million-yuan institutional-capital architecture as the two-year-cycle snapshot. From 2020 founding to mid-2026, OpenAtom has accumulated 198 donors and 600 million yuan — a six-year donor-capital trajectory that positions OpenAtom as China’s national-tier open-source foundation with a documented capital-inflow base. From an institutional economics standpoint, the donor-count / cumulative-funding pairing is a structural finding: OpenAtom’s institutional-capital architecture is now a documented metric, not a private-governance detail.

Second, it documents the Mulan license-family 6-protocol donation as the formal institutionalization of China’s bilingual-license governance architecture inside the OpenAtom Foundation. From an institutional economics standpoint, the Mulan-donation / OpenAtom-hosting pairing is a structural finding: the Mulan license family — the Chinese-characteristic open-source license family that Christopher Tong flagged as an element of China’s standards-setting project — is now institutionally hosted by the OpenAtom Foundation, not by the Mulan community itself. This advances the Mulan license family from the community-owned license to the foundation-hosted license, a governance-layer upgrade.

Third, it documents the Yi-Hong community launch as the newest sector-branded-OS-derivative in OpenHarmony’s sector-branded-derivative architecture. The Yi-Hong (instrumentation + Hong) community — covering manufacturing, energy, IoT, education — is the fourth documented sector-branded derivative in OpenHarmony’s architecture (Gong-Hong / Dian-Hong / Che-Hong / Yi-Hong). From an institutional economics standpoint, the Yi-Hong-launch / sector-branded-architecture pairing is a structural finding: OpenHarmony’s cross-sector deployment is now a documented four-derivative sector-specific architecture, each derivative branded with a Chinese industry-character prefix.

Fourth, it documents the M-Robots / LoongArch / Anolis-upgrade triple-announcement as OpenAtom’s frontier-technology expansion architecture. M-Robots addresses the commercial-robotics open-source-ecosystem gap, LoongArch addresses the domestic instruction-set open-source-ecosystem gap, and Anolis-upgrade addresses the AI-native-OS infrastructure gap. From an institutional economics standpoint, the triple-announcement / frontier-expansion pairing is a structural finding: OpenAtom’s 2026 frontier-expansion covers robotics, instruction-sets, and AI-native-OS infrastructure — three technology-frontier categories that were not covered in the foundation’s prior institutional-expansion documentation.

Fifth, it documents the YuanTu high-quality-Skills-dataset release as OpenAtom’s institutional entry into the AI-agent-data-supply layer. From an institutional economics standpoint, the YuanTu-release / data-supply-layer-entry pairing is a structural finding: the OpenAtom Foundation has now entered the AI-agent-data-supply layer as an institutional actor, alongside its prior institutional presence in the AI-model-release layer (via AtomGit-hosted models) and the AI-governance layer (via CAICT-adjacent standards bodies).

🏛️ Commentary — Three-Pillar Strategy / Sector-Braned Architecture / Standards-Framing Reception: A Three-Layer Institutional Architecture Has Now Been Documented for China’s Open-Source Program#

This cycle’s briefing documents three simultaneous institutional moves that together reveal a three-layer institutional architecture of China’s open-source program that prior briefings did not document:

  1. MIIT Liu Yulin three-pillar industrial strategy / SCIO-tier policy-discourse formalization / OpenHarmony 1.35-billion-devices institutional count (item 1) — the first SCIO-tier formalization of MIIT’s open-source industrial strategy under the three-pillar vocabulary (强供给 / 促应用 / 育生态), and the first MIIT-authorized institutional count of the OpenHarmony ecosystem at 1.35 billion devices, advanced through the SCIO channel.

  2. 2026 OpenAtom conference 198-donor / 600-million-yuan / Mulan-donation / Yi-Hong-launch / YuanTu-dataset (item 2) — the OpenAtom Foundation’s two-year-cycle institutional snapshot, documenting its 198-donor / 600-million-yuan capital architecture, the Mulan license-family 6-protocol donation (governance-layer upgrade for Chinese-characteristic bilingual licenses), the Yi-Hong community launch (fourth sector-branded-OS-derivative in OpenHarmony’s architecture), and the YuanTu dataset release (OpenAtom’s institutional entry into the AI-agent-data-supply layer).

  3. Tong’s August 12 academic article reaching Asia Times on August 17 (item 3) — the cross-surface extension of Tong’s standards-setting framing of Chinese open-source policy from The Conversation’s syndicated-academic surface to Asia Times’ international-asia-affairs surface, creating a documented two-surface institutional-reception architecture for the standards-setting framing.

The institutional reading: these three moves do not cancel each other out. They operate on three distinct institutional layers — state-council-policy-discourse (item 1), national-foundation-institutional-capital (item 2), and international-academic-reception (item 3) — and together they form a three-layer institutional architecture of China’s open-source program that prior briefings did not document.

The MIIT / SCIO / OpenAtom layer-pairing (items 1 and 2) is a state-policy / national-foundation layer-pairing: the SCIO press conference formalizes the three-pillar industrial strategy and MIIT-authorized metrics; the OpenAtom conference formalizes the foundation’s capital architecture, license-hosting role, and frontier-expansion coverage. They operate on two distinct but mutually-reinforcing institutional layers — state-policy + national-foundation. The Tong / Asia Times layer (item 3) is an international-academic-reception layer, and its cross-surface extension (The Conversation + Asia Times) is a framing-reception event rather than a policy-formalization event.

The September 30, 2026 Black Duck deadline (documented August 24, three weeks away) remains the nearest institutional milestone. This cycle’s three-layer institutional architecture — state-policy / national-foundation / international-academic-reception — is now the operating architecture of China’s open-source institutional ecosystem through at least Q4 2026. The Black Duck compliance-vacuum and the MIIT three-pillar / OpenAtom capital / Tong standards-setting moves form a layered institutional-complex: Black Duck’s exit creates a compliance-vacuum; MIIT’s three-pillar strategy formalizes the open-source industrial policy; OpenAtom’s capital architecture formalizes the foundation-governance layer; Tong’s standards-setting framing formalizes the international-academic-reception layer. Whether the four layers converge into a documented institutional-replacement architecture or diverge into a documented institutional gap is the defining institutional question for Q4 2026.
