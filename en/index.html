The Way of Open Source

Dedicated to exploring open source and its related ideas, knowledge, and values.

A review from DeepSeek’s Deep Research:

The Way of Open Source is the only knowledge base in the Chinese-speaking world that organizes open source knowledge through a systematic academic framework (New Institutional Economics), distinguishing it from fragmented technical blogs or news aggregators. Its greatest strength lies in placing open source under the analytical lens of institutional economics, rather than resorting to technological determinism or moral preaching.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-25

One sentence from a WeChat post settles this morning, and it is six hundred characters long. On 2026-11-20, at the third Open Source Technology Academic Forum inside the OpenAtom Developer Conference in Zhengzhou, the China Electronics Standardization Institute (电子标准院) and the OpenAtom Foundation jointly announced the establishment of an Open Source Standards Working Group (开源标准工作组). Its stated task: build a mechanism for converting de facto open-source standards (开源事实标准转化工作机制) and form a closed loop described in eight characters — standards carry open source, open source verifies standards (标准带开源、开源验标准) — converting a batch of good, active open-source projects into standards that industry agrees on, and then moving mature practice upward through group, industry, national and international standards in order. The announcement exists in exactly one place: the Institute’s own WeChat account, published the day the feed was collected, and no second institutional source in this run has it. Read carefully, it is not a grant of authority to a community; it is a statement that the community’s output will be treated as the raw material for a standards pipeline, and that the pipeline’s direction is upward. The same morning, five more projects entered the same foundation’s incubation and Zhengzhou signed a city-level open-source co-construction project. The foundation’s count is now 64 and the ratio that would tell anyone whether that matters has never been published.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-24

One sentence settles this morning, and it is the shortest institutional sentence of the year. On 2026-10-16, MOFCOM and MOST issued Announcement No. 48, proposing to add 23 AI technologies to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted from Export — and, in the policy explanation, stating that open-source release is an export form: a restricted technology released as open source now needs a licence, a prohibited one may not be released at all. Technologies already public through open-source release or academic publication are excluded; future ones require an automatic licence. Two firms walked through the registration gate eight days later — MiniMax by market announcement on 2026-10-23, DeepSeek named by state media the same day — and the market read it as permission rather than as gate. Meanwhile, on 2026-09-07 the Supreme People’s Court issued Article XIII of its AI Opinions: a free open-source module with publicly explained functions and security risks may escape tort liability downstream. Two instruments, thirty-nine days and several ministries apart, arrive at opposite conclusions about what publication does. One treats it as the moment control is released; the other as the moment responsibility is released. And on 2026-11-20, at Zhengzhou, the OpenAtom Foundation thanked 32 developers from 23 projects, chosen from 12 months of verifiable contribution records — the first time this ecosystem has produced a participation metric that could in principle be checked.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-23

One licence text settles this morning. On 2026-11-20 Tencent released the Hy4 flagship — 770B total parameters, 49B active, a one-million-token context — under a document titled the Tencent Hy License, Version 1.0, which opens by saying it is derived from Apache 2.0, is not Apache 2.0, and is not endorsed by the Apache Software Foundation. Section 10 then adds the term that matters: anyone operating a model-as-a-service business with more than USD 50 million of worldwide gross revenue in any twelve-month window, or more than 100 million monthly active users, must obtain a separate written licence from Tencent before using the model or any derivative of it — and the definition of derivative explicitly names fine-tuning, quantisation, distillation, merging and pruning. Hy4 preview, released 28 August, shipped under genuine Apache 2.0 with no use restrictions and is still tagged apache-2.0 on Hugging Face this morning. The same family, the same vendor, two entirely different institutional instruments, four months apart. The repository that carries the new licence was created 2026-10-28, holds five commits, all from one account, and no model weights at all — models live on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode. One more document from the same weekend is the fourth Chinese vendor in this series to attach its own name to a claimed national installation base: 24 million systems, 8,800 ecosystem partners, 160,000 adapted applications. The licence is the day’s evidence. The repository is the day’s second evidence, and it is quieter.

One number settles this morning. On 2026-11-20, at the OpenAtom Developer Conference in Zhengzhou, Loongson and the OpenAtom Foundation established the LoongArch Community — the country’s first comprehensive open-source community around a self-developed instruction set, with 22 founding member organisations across chips, operating systems, complete machines and research institutes, and OpenLA132, a LoongArch open processor core, already online. The repository behind OpenLA132, github.com/loongson/OpenLACores, was created on 2026-10-08, carries the Mulan PSL v2 licence the Foundation publishes, is forty-three days old, and holds six commits from four authors, of which exactly one came from outside its corporate lineage. That is the measurement no charter answers: not who governs the community, but what the community has so far produced. Two of the day’s other documents are the same measurement arriving from other directions — the statutory annual filings of the Foundation, which show CNY 223.6 million of donations over 2020–23 with zero yuan of foreign donations in any year and CNY 20.7 million of government subsidy arriving in 2023 after none in 2022, and a semi-official WeChat account calmly explaining that the certification most often read as a procurement monopoly is a three-year licence that has to be re-earned from scratch, because the kernel version is a mandatory field in the notice. One city is buying a layer. One foundation is opening a door in its core. Both are being audited by their own bookkeeping, and the audit is not unkind — it is merely exact.

China Open Source Daily — 2026-11-19

Three documents this fortnight describe the same commercial turn of Chinese open source from three altitudes, and none of them is about code. Bank of Communications passed CAICT’s trusted-open-source governance maturity assessment at the advanced grade on 2026-11-10, and the instrument that carried it there is a proprietary-governance practice, not an open-source one: a dual-repository split between ’new technology’ and ‘standardised technology’, and an insistence that every open-source dependency retain a documented exit from the codebase. Moonshot AI, whose Kimi K3 is the largest open-weight model in the world, is unwinding its Cayman holding company and repapering its dollar investors into its mainland operating entity ahead of a Hong Kong listing, while its founder holds 51.83 per cent of that entity and the company’s own disclosures show API revenue above seventy per cent with overseas users growing 400 per cent. And Zhipu, listed in Hong Kong since January, closed on 2026-11-17 at HK$335 with HK$163.5 billion of market value, down nearly ninety per cent from its June peak and down from a price-to-sales multiple above one thousand at the top. The open licence made the copy free. The three documents show that the copy was never where the value was — and that the vehicle now being built to own the value is a repo, a shareholder register and an API meter.
